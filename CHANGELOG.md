## [2.17.0] - 2026-Oct-03

> **What's new in a nutshell**
>
> **Notifications**: the car can now tell you what it did. A **trip summary** after every drive — the same figures the trip itself records — plus, if you want them, a note when a **charge finishes** and, for Pro, the **cell-imbalance** warning that now also appears as a notification. They arrive as messages to your own **Telegram** bot, and also as a **notification feed in the web companion**. No server, no account, no second app. Each summary carries the **number** its trip or charge has in History, opens with its **start and end time**, shows the battery level from your chosen **SoC source** (BMS with its decimal, or the dashboard's whole percent), and one that couldn't be sent — parked in an underground garage, say — goes out **the moment the car is back online**. Telegram now lives with the other connections, in **Settings → Connections**.
>
> The companion also gains a **Files** tab, so backups, exports and the diagnostics log can be pulled off the car — or a backup pushed onto it — from the phone in your hand, with nothing plugged in, and the **48-hour 12V / HV battery chart** from the dashboard.
>
> And **remote access**: the car can now join your own private **Tailscale** network, which makes the **web companion** and **ADB** reachable from anywhere — at work, on holiday, over mobile data while the car is turned off — without opening a single port on your router. Tap **Sign in with your phone** under **Settings → Connections → Tailscale**, scan the QR code and approve the sign-in on your phone — nothing to type on the car and no key to copy — and that is the whole setup.
>
> Also, a new **DiLink-3** option to **keep Wi-Fi alive when the car is off**. The head unit switches Wi-Fi off a few minutes after you switch the car off, which takes the **web companion**, adb and any LAN tools offline until the car is next used — even though the unit is still on and reporting over mobile data. With this turned on, the app's privileged background helper re-enables Wi-Fi whenever it drops while the car is off — the same approach the third-party keepalive apps use — so the car stays reachable on your home network without one of those installed. It only acts while the car is off, and backs off when the **12V battery** or **state of charge** is low so it can't flatten the battery. Off by default; find it under **Settings → App → Power & background**, and it needs the same one-time ADB setup as instant telemetry.
>
> For **DiLink-5**, a long-running mystery is solved: the car switches off Android's debugging settings as it shuts down, which left the app's background helper — the piece behind **instant speed and power**, and behind restarting the app after the car force-stops it — with no way back in after every overnight reboot. It could not repair itself, because that same connection was what it would have needed to do the repairing. The app now switches those settings back on **by itself** within seconds of starting, which means **ADB now stays on across reboots** there, as it was when you did the one-time ADB setup. **DiLink-3 is untouched** — it never loses the connection, so the app never writes to it.
>
> And on **DiLink-5** and newer head units, the app can **install its own updates** again. On those cars an update meant downloading the APK on a computer and sideloading it over ADB, because the installer an ordinary app is given cannot complete there. The app now installs through the same helper connection instant telemetry already uses, so **Download update** and **Install now** work as they do on DiLink-3 — with the usual backup taken first. That connection is what decides it: without the one-time ADB setup there is still nothing on the car that can install anything, and the manual instructions appear instead.
>
> **Trips on DiLink-5 are now recorded as they were driven.** DiLink-5 doesn't tell the app whether the car is switched on, so every slow-down in traffic looked like the car being switched off, and each time the app stopped recording for the next half-minute — a 20-minute drive could show as 12, and a wait of more than three minutes split the drive into two trips. The app now tells a switch-off from a wait by what the car does to it — switching off closes the app within seconds — so a stop counts as part of the trip for as long as the car stays on. Trips already recorded get their durations corrected once, automatically — and again after restoring a backup. And on DiLink-3 cars that never tell the app they're switched on, a wait in **P** with the power on no longer ends the trip either. Two faults that turned up alongside it are fixed on every head unit and put right in your history the same way: **trips that never moved** — a 3-minute, 0 km trip recorded whenever the car woke by itself, often in the middle of the night — and **energy figures of minus 16.7 million kWh** on the first drive of some mornings.
>
> **Charging History** is more honest too. The battery reading that sags while you drive creeps back up once the car rests, and the app used to record that rebound as a small **"Reconstructed" charge** of about 1 kWh — after a few hours in an underground garage, with no charger in sight. That no longer happens. On **DiLink-5**, charging is now **detected at all** — the app was reading the car's charging power but never counting it as a charge, so no session was ever recorded there. And each charge's **cost** now sits next to the **energy it bought**, instead of next to the distance driven since the previous charge, where *"227 km since last charge · €1.67"* read as though those kilometres had cost €1.67. And **ABRP** now gets the same **battery percentage your dashboard shows** — it was being sent the battery system's own reading, which on some cars runs 2–3 points lower. And both histories now show the reading you chose under **SoC source** at its own precision — the battery system's with its decimal, the dashboard's in whole numbers — where trip cards had been rounding the first and charging cards adding a decimal to the second.
>
> **Backups are smaller, and restoring one is now safe.** Every backup is **gzip-compressed** — a database of several hundred MB typically shrinks to about a twentieth of that, so backups take less room on the car and the SD card, cost less mobile data and fit **Telegram**'s limit for much longer — and older `.db` backups restore exactly as before. Restore itself got an overhaul after one wiped a test car's history: a backup taken while the app was recording could come out damaged inside, nothing checked for it, and the damaged database was then **deleted** the first time the app wrote to it. Backups are now taken from a **frozen snapshot**, every backup is **checked end to end** before it is restored, the confirmation shows **what the backup holds next to what is on the car**, your current data is **saved first** so a wrong pick can be undone, and a damaged database is **kept for recovery** instead of deleted. After updating, the app also **checks its database once** and tells you if an earlier restore left damage behind. The progress and result of a backup or restore now show **at the top of the screen, wherever you've scrolled to**, and every trip and charge carries its **number** (`#756`) on its card.
>
> And **Settings** is easier to find your way around. **App** and **Preferences** now open the way **Connections** does — an overview of cards that show where each part stands, every one opening on a page of its own — instead of one long scroll each. The **web companion** moved to **Connections**, beside Tailscale, and the Pro **cell-imbalance alert** to **Preferences → Battery**. And on the dashboard, the **Distance** tile now works like the battery tile: tap it to switch between this session's distance and the **distance since your last charge**.

### Added

- **Tap the Distance tile to see the distance since your last charge** — the dashboard's Distance tile (Classic and Cards layouts) now works like the battery tile: tap it to switch from this session's distance to the **distance driven since the last charge**, and tap again to switch back. It is measured on the odometer from where the most recent charge began — the same point Charging History measures its *since last charge* figure from — so it reads 0 while you are charging, and shows a dash until there is a charge to measure from. It only changes what the tile shows; the choice is remembered, and travels with the settings backup.

- **Notifications** — the car can now tell you what it did, with no server, no account and no second app to install. Three events, each switched on or off **separately** under **Settings → Connections → Telegram Notifications**: a **trip summary** after every drive — distance, duration, average speed, energy and consumption measured against your lifetime average, state of charge used, cost and trip score; **charging finished**, when a session ends — whether it completed or stopped short, at what level, with the energy added and what it cost; and, with **Pro**, the **cell-imbalance alert**, which until now only appeared on the car's own screen.

  Every number in the summary is read back from the recorded trip and priced the same way the trip screen prices it, so the card and the trip in the app always agree — including the cost, which follows the real price of the charges that energy came from rather than a flat average. The charging card works the same way, and waits out the minutes in which the car settles the last percent or so of a near-full charge, so it never says 98% about a charge the app then records as 100%.

  **Charging is off by default**, on its own switch, and that is deliberate: it is the one event whose timing you don't choose. An overnight charge that completes at 03:00 sends at 03:00, so whether you want to be told is your call to make, separately from trip summaries.

  A summary goes out **when the trip ends** — which is once the car has been off for the **auto-stop time** in *Settings → Preferences → Trip recording*, three minutes by default, so a longer setting simply means a later message. On **DiLink-3** in the default **Always On** background mode the app is still running at that point, so summaries arrive shortly after you walk away from the car. A trip the app could only close **afterwards** — because it wasn't running when you parked, which on **DiLink-5** is every trip left to close on its own, since the car force-stops the app at ignition-off — is still recorded in full, but not announced: being buzzed about yesterday's drive as you set off on today's is worse than not being told at all. On DiLink-5 that makes automatic summaries unavailable for now; ending the trip yourself with **Stop** before switching the car off closes it live and does send one.

  The same events also appear in the **web companion**, under a bell in the header: a feed of what the car has recently done, with a count of what you haven't read yet. That feed is independent of Telegram — it works with no bot linked at all, because it is the car's own history rather than a copy of what a bot was told to forward. It is a feed rather than a phone-style push for a reason worth stating plainly: the companion is served over plain HTTP from the car's own address on your network, and browsers only permit real notifications on a secure (HTTPS) origin. Where the companion *is* served securely, it will also raise a proper system notification after you allow it.

  If the car has no connection at the moment an event happens — parked in an underground garage, say — the message is **queued and sent the moment the connection comes back**, with a few timed retries on top; the queue survives the app being killed, and a message is dropped only once it is more than a day old. Every trip and charging summary **carries its History number in the title** and **starts with its own start and end time** (the same ones History shows), so one that arrives late still says which drive or charge it describes, and its battery figures follow your **SoC source** in *Settings → Preferences → Battery*: **BMS** shows one decimal and says so, **Panel** shows whole percent, as the trip screens do. **Off by default**; a **Send test message** button proves the wiring before you rely on it.

- **Remote access to the car, with Tailscale** — the web companion has always been home-network-only: leave the house and the address in Settings stops working. The car can now join **your own private Tailscale network**, which makes the companion **and ADB** reachable from anywhere, over mobile data, including while the car is turned off — with nothing exposed to the internet and no ports opened on your router.

  Setup involves **no typing on the car at all**. **Settings → Connections → Tailscale → Sign in with your phone** puts a **QR code** on screen: scan it, approve the sign-in in your phone's browser, and the car joins your network. There is no auth key to generate, copy or lose — and nothing long-lived that could leak. (If you prefer one, *Other ways to sign in* still takes an auth key, and tells you how to type it from your computer with `adb shell input text` — which you already have set up, since Tailscale needs the same authorisation.) The car joins your network, keeps the same address from then on, and the app shows you that address with the companion and ADB commands ready to copy. Step-by-step instructions are in the app itself, under *How to set this up*, including the three admin-console settings that quietly cause trouble later — **disable key expiry** for the car (or it silently drops off your network in about six months, and only a trip to the car fixes it), **no exit node** on the car (it would push its ABRP, MQTT and Telegram traffic through someone else's internet connection), and **no Funnel** (it would put the companion on the public internet behind only its PIN).

  You do **not** need the Tailscale app on the head unit — the app carries the Tailscale daemon itself and runs it through the same one-time ADB authorisation that instant telemetry uses. It runs without a VPN permission, without root, and it adds about 5 MB to the download. If you'd rather not use it, it costs nothing: it only ever runs once you have signed the car in.

  Two side effects worth knowing. This is also the gentlest answer to the **parked** problem — the head unit stays on mobile data after the car cuts Wi-Fi, so ADB, the companion and MQTT to a broker on your tailnet all keep working while parked **without touching the Wi-Fi radio**. And on **DiLink-5**, the head unit powers itself down about ten minutes after you switch the car off, so remote access lasts until then and returns when the car next wakes.

- **Files in the web companion** — a new **Files** tab that lists a handful of folders on the head unit: **App files** (the diagnostics log, HTML exports), **Backups (Download)**, **Screenshots** taken on the car, **Backups (SD card)** if a card is in, and the automatic rolling backups the app keeps to itself. Download any of them straight to the phone or laptop you are holding, read the **diagnostics log in the browser** without downloading it first, and upload a file — a database backup to restore, say — to the car without plugging anything in. Over **Tailscale** that works from anywhere, which makes "send me your diag.log" a thing you can do from the sofa.

  **View** opens files inside the companion, with a Back button. Text files get a **Copy** button that puts what's on screen on your clipboard, ready to paste into a bug report; a multi-MB `diag.log` is shown from the end (the last 256 KB, which is the part you're looking for) rather than refused for its size, and rotated names like `diag.log.prev` count as logs too. **Screenshots open full-size** in the page, so getting one off the car is a look and a tap rather than a download and a hunt.

  Files can also be **deleted** — old backups pile up — with a confirmation first. Only files, never folders, and only in folders the app can actually write to: the automatic backups stay read-only because the app prunes those itself, and a shared folder the app lacks the **Storage permission** for now says so up front, with where to grant it, instead of offering a Delete button that fails. (Android grants reading and writing shared storage separately, so a folder can list perfectly and refuse every delete.) ADB setup now also grants that permission while it has the shell open.

  It is deliberately **not** a browser for the whole head unit. Only those folders are listed, the automatic backups are read-only, and everything sits behind the companion's **PIN** — because the companion also answers on the car's Wi-Fi, and a database backup is your complete trip history. If you need wider access, ADB over the tailnet already gives it to you.

- **The web companion now shows every address it answers on** — it used to show *one*, whichever network interface the system happened to list first, so on a car with Tailscale running you had a 50/50 chance of being shown the address that doesn't work from where you are standing. Each address is now listed and labelled — *On your network*, *Over Tailscale — works from anywhere* — with its own copy button. The **Tailnet address** also appears in App Diagnostics, so a "can't reach it" report carries its own answer.

- **The web companion can be encrypted over Tailscale** — a new **Serve over HTTPS** switch hands the companion to the Tailscale daemon, which listens on 443 for the car, terminates TLS with a real **Let's Encrypt** certificate for its Tailscale name, and passes requests to the local server. The address becomes `https://<car>.<tailnet>.ts.net/` — with no port on the end, unlike the plain-http ones — so browsers stop flagging it as insecure — and the things browsers only permit on a secure page begin to work, notifications and copy-to-clipboard among them. The certificate is fetched and renewed by the daemon; there is nothing to install, and no port is opened on your router.

  Getting there needed one fix to the bundled daemon: Android has no `/etc/resolv.conf` and the daemon is built without a libc fallback, so it had no working DNS and the certificate fetch failed before it began. The tailnet itself never showed this, because that connects without DNS. The daemon now reads the head unit's nameservers from Android's system properties.

  It needs **MagicDNS** and **HTTPS Certificates** enabled for your tailnet — two one-time toggles in the admin console, free, applying to your whole network — and the app spells out where to find them under *How to turn this on for your network*, including the genuinely confusing bit that MagicDNS is **on** when the button offers to *disable* it. If they're missing, the switch shows the daemon's own refusal rather than failing silently. Only the `.ts.net` name is encrypted: a certificate can't be issued for a bare `100.x` address, so that one and the WiFi address stay `http://`. This grew the bundled daemon from 5.31 MB to **5.58 MB**.

- **The companion no longer lists an address that can't work** — a car on mobile data has a private `10.x` address on its modem, and the address list took "private range" to mean "your network" and offered it as a third place to connect. It's behind carrier NAT, so nothing could ever reach it. Addresses are now matched to the interface carrying them, and a carrier-NAT mobile address is left out. The same fix stops a carrier that hands out CGNAT addresses from having its modem labelled "Over Tailscale — works from anywhere".

- **The Tailscale card shows the name you gave the car** — renaming the machine in the Tailscale admin console had no visible effect here, because the card showed the name the *app* reports about itself rather than the machine name the console sets. It now reads the latter, falling back to the reported name on a tailnet with MagicDNS turned off. The name the app registers under by default has also lost its **`byd-`** prefix — it is a prefix nobody chose, and on a head unit whose model already says BYD it came out as `byd-byd-…`. Renaming in the console still wins over both, since Tailscale stops following the device's own name once you have renamed it there.

- **The 12V / HV battery history is in the web companion** — a 🔋 button beside the bell opens the same **48-hour** chart the dashboard's HV / 12V card does: 12V on the left axis against SoC on the right, charging periods shaded, and the Latest / Min / Max / Δ figures above it, plus the current HV pack voltage. SoC follows your Panel-or-BMS setting, resolved on the car, so the companion and the app plot the same line.

  The whole window is sent rather than thinned down — a brief 12V sag is the thing this chart exists to catch, and averaging points away would smooth it out. Gaps in the line are stretches where the app wasn't running and nothing was recorded; with **Always On** and the parked **Wi-Fi keepalive** on a DiLink-3 there aren't any, which is what makes checking it from away from the car worth doing.

- **Web companion on a phone** — the header no longer pushes the load button off the right edge: below phone width the tabs take a full row of their own beneath the logo and the bell, sharing it evenly, and the wordmark gives way to the icon on the narrowest screens. **Load Database** is now **Load DB**. Each list's totals move onto a single line under its title — `471 trips · 10,124 km · 1,954 kWh` — instead of competing with the heading for the same row. The sort-direction, favourites and filters controls are now icons rather than labelled pills, with the words moved to their tooltips and a **count badge** on Filters so a filtered list still says so.

- **Keep Wi-Fi alive when car is off (DiLink-3)** — a new toggle in **Settings → App → Power & background** that stops the head unit from becoming unreachable on your local network after you switch the car off. BYD's MCU cuts the Wi-Fi module minutes after the car is switched off, so the **web companion**, adb and LAN-based MQTT all go dark until the next drive, even though the unit stays powered on mobile data. When enabled, the app's privileged background helper re-enables Wi-Fi whenever it drops while the car is off — a plain radio toggle, the same mechanism Electro/Overdrive use — so you can reach the car on Wi-Fi without a third-party keepalive app. It **only acts while the car is off** (it never overrides a Wi-Fi-off you set while driving) and **skips when the 12V auxiliary battery or the traction state-of-charge is low**, to protect the battery on a long park. **Off by default**; requires the advanced ADB setup (the same one instant telemetry uses). DiLink-5 is unaffected — it powers its head unit down shortly after standby regardless.

- **DiLink-5 and newer head units can install their own updates again** — on these cars the About tab could tell you a new version existed and then leave you to fetch it: download the APK on a PC, and run `adb install -r` at the car. The silent installer an ordinary Android app is given cannot complete there, and there is no installer screen on the head unit to fall back to, so the buttons were withheld rather than offered and left to fail.

  The app now installs through the same **privileged helper connection** it already uses for instant telemetry, which can run a real install on the car. **Download update** and **Install now** behave exactly as they do on DiLink-3 — including the **database backup taken before every install**, and the rule that the button only appears once you are parked with no trip or charging session in progress.

  It needs the **one-time ADB setup**, the same one instant telemetry needs — nothing new to grant, and nothing to do if you have already done it. Without that connection nothing on the car can install anything, so the manual instructions stay in place rather than a button that cannot work; their wording now says the connection isn't available, instead of suggesting your head unit will never update itself. And if a privileged install is attempted and fails, the manual card returns rather than a second attempt down a route already known not to work.

  Verified end-to-end on a Sealion 7, including replacing the app **while it was running**: the system performs the swap itself, and the telemetry service comes back on its own afterwards. The app's own window drops to the launcher, which is expected — you do not need to reopen it for recording to carry on.

### Changed

- **Database backups are now compressed** — every backup (Download folder, SD card, Telegram, the scheduled Telegram backup, and the safety copy taken before an in-app update installs) is now saved as a gzip-compressed `.db.gz` instead of a raw `.db`. A trip database typically shrinks to a fraction of its size, so backups take less space on the car and the SD card, cost less mobile data, and stay under **Telegram's 50 MB limit** for much longer — the limit is now checked against the compressed file, not the raw database. **Restoring from Telegram gains even more**: Telegram lets a bot download files of at most **20 MB**, so until now any backup over 20 MB could be sent to your chat but never restored from the in-app list. Trip databases compress about 15–18×, which moves that ceiling from a 20 MB database to one of roughly 300 MB. A backup that still compresses to more than 20 MB has to take the long way: save it from the chat on your phone, upload it to *Backups (Download)* in the web companion's **Files** tab, and restore it from the backup list. **Restore reads both formats**: older `.db` backups, files pushed over ADB and backups from other devices restore exactly as before. The format is detected from the file's contents rather than its name, and a damaged or truncated archive is rejected before the live database is touched. The **web companion**'s *Load backup* viewer opens both formats too. To open a new backup in a SQLite browser on your PC, decompress it first (`gunzip file.db.gz`, or 7-Zip on Windows).

- **Trips and charging sessions show their number** — each card in **Trip History** and **Charging History** now shows its id (`#756`) just right of where the selection checkbox appears, in a fixed slot: it doesn't move when you start selecting, and the dates stay lined up from card to card. Handy when a trip or charge needs pointing at in a bug report.

- **Backup & Restore reports where you can see it** — the progress, result or error of a backup or restore appeared at the very top of the **Backup & Restore** list, while the buttons that start them sit well down it, so the message could come and go scrolled out of sight — an SD card backup could fail with *"Not enough space"* and nothing visible happened. The message is now pinned just under the title bar, wherever the list is scrolled to, and the list keeps its place.

- **Restore shows what you'll get, and keeps a copy of what you had** — after you pick a backup — from the list, from Telegram or from a file — the app checks it and then shows how many trips and charging sessions it holds and over which dates, next to what is on the car now, with a warning in red when the backup has fewer or older trips than you have. Confirming first saves your current database to `Download/BydTripStats` as `byd_stats_before_restore_….db.gz`, so a wrong pick is undone by restoring that copy. (Choosing a file in the file browser used to start the restore straight away, with no confirmation at all.)

- **Each connection now opens on its own page** — **Settings → Connections** used to put everything behind one *Open Connections* button, which then stacked ABRP, MQTT, Telegram and Tailscale into a single long scroll: four connections' worth of switches, tokens and status rows to get past to reach the one you came for. The cards **are** the navigation now — tap one and you get that connection and nothing else, with a back arrow to the overview. The **web companion** has joined them, beside Tailscale (see *Settings reorganised* below). The cards still show live status at a glance, so the overview answers "is MQTT connected?" without opening anything. They sit **two across** on the head unit's wide screen, and fall back to **one per row** when there isn't the width for that — rotated, or sharing the screen with another app.

- **Telegram moved to Settings → Connections** — linking the bot is setting up a connection, and it now sits with **ABRP**, **MQTT** and the web companion rather than inside Backup & Restore, which is where it happened to be first used. Backup & Restore keeps everything that is actually about backups — the schedule, **Send Backup Now** and the restore list — and points at Connections when no bot is linked yet, as do the diagnostics and trip-export buttons that can also send through it. One bot, linked in one place, used by several features.

- **DiLink-5 now says which connections stop when the car does** — **MQTT** and **ABRP** kept quiet about something the Tailscale and web-companion pages already explained: on DiLink-5 the head unit powers itself down within about ten minutes of the car being switched off, and nothing runs after that, so there is nothing left to publish. A parked car that stops reporting looks exactly like a broken broker or a bad token, and it was reasonable to spend an evening on TLS settings and topics before suspecting the head unit had simply switched itself off. Both pages now say so up front, including what it means in practice — a gap in Home Assistant between parking and the next drive, and parked charging sessions that reach the app but not ABRP live. Neither feature is restricted: while the car is on, both behave exactly as they do on DiLink-3, and both resume on their own when the car next wakes.

- **Charging History: a charge's cost now sits next to the energy it bought** — each session card showed the cost on the same line as the distance driven since the previous charge, so *"227 km since last charge · ~€1.67"* read as though those 227 km had cost €1.67. They hadn't: €1.67 is the price of the **12.40 kWh added in that charge**, and most of the 227 km ran on energy from earlier charges. The cost now follows the kWh (*12.40 kWh · ~€1.67*), as it does on the trip cards, and the distance has a line of its own. What your driving actually cost is what each trip's own cost already shows.

- **Settings reorganised: App and Preferences open as cards, like Connections** — both tabs were one long scroll. **App** stacked the power settings, backups, the compatibility probe, the diagnostics charts with their ADB shell, and the web companion one after another; **Preferences** ran to eleven cards under four headings. Each now opens on an overview of cards that say where things stand — *Always On • Wi-Fi keepalive on*, *Last backup: …*, *Dark • Classic*, *Auto-stop after 3 min • no minimum* — and tapping one opens that topic on a page of its own, with a back arrow to the overview. The cards are compact enough that a whole tab fits on the head unit's screen without scrolling, and the system back gesture returns to the overview first, rather than leaving Settings from the middle of a page.

  - **App**: *Power & background*, *Backup & Restore* (which goes straight to its existing screen), *Vehicle Compatibility* and *App Diagnostics*.
  - **Preferences**: *Appearance* (theme, dashboard layout, dashboard icons & animations), *Language & units*, *Trip recording* (auto-stop time, ask before auto-stopping, minimum trip distance), *Battery* (SoC source and the cell-imbalance alert), *Costs* (electricity tariff and currency) and *Goals & personal bests* (straight to the Goals screen).

  A few things changed places. The **web companion** is now under **Connections**, beside **Tailscale** — the switch that serves it over HTTPS was already on the Tailscale card. The **Pro cell-imbalance alert** is under **Preferences → Battery**: it was the only actual setting on the Pro tab, among cards that otherwise just describe Pro features, and it is now shown and locked the way the Neon theme and the Cards layout already are, with the Pro tab keeping a card that says what it does. The **Power & background** controls (the *Always On / Minimal / Deep Sleep* mode and the new *Keep Wi-Fi alive when car is off* toggle) sit on **App** rather than **Preferences**, since they are system behaviours rather than display settings — and on **DiLink-5**, so does *Allow reading vehicle data*, which changes a head-unit system setting. The Always On description was also clarified, and the Minimal wake-alarm status line now shows only in Minimal mode (it was confusingly displayed, and named, on the other modes too).

  Smaller things along the way: on **Vehicle Compatibility** and **App Diagnostics**, the three ways to send a report — **Telegram**, **Save** and **Email via QR code** — now sit side by side on one row, and the probe's **delete** button sits beside the switch that records the data rather than among the ways of sending it. The text under the diagnostics buttons now says what each of the three does — it used to say the log went only to Telegram and was never saved locally, beside a Save button. And **Disconnect** on the Telegram and Tailscale pages is a proper button rather than a text link.

  **Opening Settings no longer restarts the web companion.** Its settings used to sit on the tab Settings opens on, and showing them stopped and restarted the companion's server every time — so a page loading in a browser at that moment failed. That now happens only when you open the Web Companion page itself.

- **Home Assistant: the sensor setup is sent only when something has changed** — Home Assistant builds the car's entities from 49 discovery messages the app leaves on your MQTT broker, and the app sent the whole set again every time it started. On **DiLink-5**, where the car closes the app at every switch-off, that meant several times a day: one Sealion 7 sent it 8 times in two days, about 20 KB each time, describing sensors that hadn't changed since the app was installed. The app now remembers what it last sent, and to which broker, and sends it again only when it changes — an update that adds or corrects a sensor, a new friendly name, or a different broker. A broker that loses what it was holding (Mosquitto without persistence, a recreated container) is something the car cannot see, so two safety nets cover it: the set is sent again **once a day** regardless, and whenever **Home Assistant announces a restart** on `homeassistant/status`. Two older faults went with it. **Switching to a different broker** could leave it with the readings but not the setup — saving the new address with **Save** rather than **Test & Save** was enough — so Home Assistant showed no entities until the app next restarted; the new broker now gets the setup straight away. And **Test & Save** now always sends the setup again, as the Home Assistant guide says it does, where before only the first press after opening the page did — it is the remedy for a broker that has lost it. Found with a logging broker on a Sealion 7 and contributed by **[@luads](https://github.com/luads)** ([#25](https://github.com/angoikon/byd-trip-stats/pull/25)).

### Fixed

- **A restore could wipe your trip history — days after it seemed to have worked** — three faults lined up. A backup is a copy of the database file, taken while the app keeps recording, and if the car was driving or charging at the time the file could change halfway through the copy: the result looked like any other backup but had a broken table inside, and restore only checked the file's first 16 bytes. Once restored, the damage sat unnoticed until the app next wrote to that table — a charging session, in the case that found this — and Android's standard response to a damaged database is to **delete it**; the app then started again with an empty one. All three are fixed. **Backups are taken from a frozen snapshot**: recording carries on, but nothing can change the file while it's being copied. **Every backup is checked end to end before it is restored**, and a damaged one is refused with a message instead of installed. And **a database found damaged is never deleted again**: it is moved aside and saved to `Download/BydTripStats` as `byd_stats_damaged_….sqlite.gz`, where its data can still be recovered. Restoring no longer races the recorder either — the app stops recording first and swaps the new file in in one step, so a restore that is interrupted leaves your previous data intact instead of half-overwritten. That also ends two crashes: one when a restore closed the database while a charging session was still writing to it, and one when a damaged database was deleted halfway through saving a charge. The backup taken before an in-app update no longer closes the database under the recorder either. And because a damaged backup restored with an earlier beta can still be sitting in the database unnoticed, **the app checks its database once after this update**, in the background about two minutes after it starts: if it finds damage, it saves a copy to `Download/BydTripStats` and tells you with a notification, so you can restore a good backup before the app next touches the damaged part.

- **"Not enough space on the SD card" when there was plenty** — since backups became compressed, the SD card check still asked for room for the *uncompressed* database — hundreds of MB — rather than the backup itself, about a twentieth of that. The backup is now compressed first and the card checked against its real size, and a card that fills up part-way no longer keeps a half-written file.

- **ABRP showed a lower battery percentage than the car** — ABRP was always sent the battery system's own (BMS) reading, whatever you had chosen under **SoC source**, while the dashboard, trip history and Telegram summaries follow that choice — and the default, **Panel**, is the percentage the car itself displays. The two readings aren't the same: on a Sealion 7 the BMS one runs 2–3 points below the dashboard between 80% and 90%, so the car and the app said 80% while ABRP said 78% ([#24](https://github.com/angoikon/byd-trip-stats/issues/24)). ABRP now receives the reading you've chosen, which by default is the dashboard figure — also what ABRP's own documentation asks for. If your car doesn't report a dashboard figure, ABRP gets the BMS reading instead of 0%. It was never limited to DiLink-5: every car sent the BMS reading, and how far it differed depended on the car. Home Assistant over **MQTT** is unaffected — it has always received both, as `soc` (BMS) and `soc_panel`.

- **History showed the battery reading at the wrong precision** — the **SoC source** setting (*Settings → Preferences → Battery*) chooses between the battery system's reading, which has a decimal, and the dashboard's, which doesn't, and History didn't always keep the two apart. With **BMS** selected, each **Trip History** card was labelled *SoC (BMS)* but showed whole numbers — `37% → 36%` for a trip that went from 37.2 to 36.0 — which is exactly how the dashboard reading looks, and the **Charging History** summary rounded its average gain the same way. With **Panel** selected it went the other way: each **Charging History** card, and a charge's own screen, gave the dashboard reading a decimal it doesn't have — `38.0% → 80.0%`. All of them now follow the rule the trip screen and the Telegram summaries already did: **BMS** with one decimal, **Panel** in whole percent.

- **DiLink-5: the car was switching off its own debugging connection at every shutdown** — and that turned out to be the reason so much stopped working there. The app's background helper — the piece behind **instant speed and power**, and behind restarting the app after the car force-stops it at ignition-off — reaches the system through a local connection on the head unit itself. On DiLink-5 that connection was **dead after every overnight reboot**, because the car clears Android's two debugging settings as it shuts down. Nothing the app did afterwards could recover it: the helper is what the app would have used to switch them back on, and it needs the connection to run.

  The app now switches them back on **itself**, in its own process, in the first moments after it starts — no helper required, which is the essential part. One tester's log shows both settings reading **off** at a 02:31 reboot, the app turning them on at **16 seconds**, and the connection alive **3 seconds later**; his four previous nightly reboots had each left it dead. On that car it used to come back only hours later, when the car was next driven, which is long after the moment it was needed.

  On some boots the head unit starts the app a few seconds earlier than usual, before it has unlocked the app's own storage. Depending on the exact moment, that either **crashed the app** outright — rescued only by its own restart a few seconds later — or let it run on with the step that switches the settings back on failed without a trace. On one Sealion 7 the second case left the app running all morning with nothing able to bring it back after the next switch-off, so that day it didn't start on its own. The same kind of early start also showed BYD switching the settings off **a second time**, at that unlock. The app now waits for the unlock before doing anything, then switches the settings on and starts its background helper, checks again whenever the connection is down for the rest of the boot, and every 15 minutes after that — so one missed attempt no longer costs the day. `diag.log` also keeps the lines written before the car's storage is ready instead of losing them, which is why this one was hard to see (`… before user unlock — start-up waits for it`, `adb re-assert: …`).

  This is done only when the connection is actually down, so a healthy head unit is never written to — **DiLink-3 keeps its connection open and is untouched**. It also needs no new permission: the app has held the one required since the ADB setup was introduced. `diag.log` records what happened and what changed, so a car that still has trouble says so in a line you can read (`adb assert: …`). One setting is the exception, written whether the connection is up or not but only on Android 11 and later: the app turns off Android's **seven-day expiry of the ADB authorisation**, so a car left unused for more than a week doesn't come back needing the one-time setup redone. It is written only when it isn't off already, and `diag.log` notes when it is (`adb key expiry: off …`). DiLink-3 runs Android 10, which has no such expiry, so it stays untouched.

  What that means for your car, stated plainly: on DiLink-5, **ADB now stays switched on across reboots**. That is the state you put the head unit in for the one-time ADB setup — the car used to undo it overnight, and the app now puts it back. It happens only on a car where that setup was completed, and new DiLink-5 installs are told so up front, in the one-time vehicle-data question. It also means that switching ADB off yourself no longer sticks while the app is installed: the app cannot tell your choice apart from the car's overnight reset, so it turns ADB back on the next time it starts.

  The app also now reports the state of that setting and of its own start-up receiver in diagnostics, which is what made the cause findable at all: two different failures — a connection that was switched off, and a start-up broadcast the car had suppressed — had until now looked identical in a log.

- **DiLink-5: trips were recorded shorter than they were driven, and a long wait split one drive into two** — a 20-minute drive could appear as 12 minutes, while its distance and energy were right. DiLink-5 doesn't tell the app whether the car is switched on, or which gear it is in — the "P" the app showed at a standstill was a placeholder, not a reading — so the app judged it from speed and power alone, and every slow-down in traffic looked exactly like the car being switched off. The app then dropped to the relaxed rhythm it uses for a parked car, looking every 30 seconds instead of every second, and by its next look the car was usually back up to speed. Each of those half-minutes went unrecorded and was later subtracted from the trip as parked time: on one Sealion 7 that was about 30% of all its recorded driving time. Distance and energy come from the car's own counters, which is why only the duration, the average speed and the detail in the speed chart suffered. A wait of more than three minutes — a long red light, a level crossing, a queue — went further: the car-off auto-stop ended the trip, and driving off started a new one. On the same car that happened 19 times; the two checked against its log were both a single drive with the car on throughout.

  What the app now uses instead: when a DiLink-5 car is switched off, it **closes the app within seconds** (and the background helper normally opens it again a few seconds later). So as long as the app has kept running since the car came to a stop, the car has not been switched off, and **a stop counts as part of the trip however long it lasts** — no auto-stop, no "End trip?" prompt, no split — as a red light always has on DiLink-3. A real switch-off is recognised by the app having been closed: the trip then ends three minutes later exactly as before, or simply carries on if you drive off within those three minutes. A trip in progress is sampled every second throughout, and the live figures on the dashboard follow the same rule as the stored trip. As a safety net, a standstill of more than 30 minutes with the app still running is handed back to the normal car-off timeout. None of this applies to DiLink-3, which reads the car's own power position instead — see the next entry.

  **Trips already recorded on DiLink-5 are corrected once, automatically**, shortly after the update — and again whenever a backup is restored, so a history brought back after reinstalling the app is corrected too. Past recordings carry no trace of whether the app was running, so the correction uses a fixed rule instead: a gap of up to 2 minutes inside a trip counts as driving. It can only make a trip longer, never shorter, and it is the best estimate there is rather than a measurement — the missing half-minutes were never recorded. On the car above it corrects 103 trips, and the drive that showed 12 minutes comes back as 21.7 — a few of them spent parked at the destination with the car still on. Drives that were already split in two stay as two trips, because a split can't be told apart from a real stop afterwards; **Merge** in History joins them. `diag.log` now records every standstill of a minute or more during a trip (`standstill during trip … lasted Ns`) and every time the car was switched off and on again mid-trip (`car-off window during trip … lasted Ns`), so a duration that still looks wrong can be explained from the log alone.

- **DiLink-3: a trip no longer ends while you sit in P with the car on** — on some DiLink-3 cars the app never receives the car's on/off state, so once you shifted into **P** with the power still on, nothing told it the car was on: no speed, no power, and a gear that is neither D nor R looked exactly like a switched-off car. After the auto-stop time (three minutes by default) the trip ended if the app wasn't on screen — or asked "End trip?" if it was — even though you hadn't switched off. (Until May the default was 30 minutes, so it rarely came up.) The app now reads the car's **power position** instead — *off*, *accessory* or *on*, the same value other BYD apps rely on — whenever the on/off state is missing: *on* keeps the trip going through any wait in P, *off* or *accessory* lets the usual auto-stop run once you switch off. Home Assistant's **Car On** sensor follows the same value, so on these cars it now reads *on* while you're parked with the power on. Cars that do report their on/off state carry on exactly as before, and DiLink-5 keeps its own rule (above). A car that turns out not to expose the power position either behaves as before, and `diag.log` now shows what it reads (`🚗 bodywork poll: …`).

- **Trips that never moved: 3-minute, 0 km trips recorded while nobody was near the car** — often in the middle of the night. The head unit wakes by itself from time to time, the app starts with it, and for the first moments there is no odometer reading yet, which the app held as 0. The app decided whether the car had moved by comparing the odometer with its previous reading, so a 0 followed by the real figure looked like driving; three minutes later the car-off auto-stop closed a trip with nothing in it. Movement now only counts when both readings are real. It was seen on DiLink-5, but the check is the same on every head unit, so the fix applies everywhere.

  **Existing ghost trips are removed once, automatically**, and again after a backup is restored. Only trips with the exact signature are touched: started automatically, 0 km, no energy used, and no more than two recorded points. A trip you started by hand, favourited or tagged is kept, and so are genuinely short drives — moving the car within a car park, say — because those record more than two points. The `trip start` line in `diag.log` now also says what started each trip (`reason=…`), which is how a trip nobody remembers driving gets explained next time.

- **Energy figures of minus 16.7 million kWh** — for a moment after waking, the car can report its lifetime energy counter as **16,777,215**, the value BYD uses for "not available". That number became the starting point of the day's first trip, so the trip recorded roughly −16.7 million kWh and an absurd consumption, and dragged every average that included it along with it. The value is now rejected where it is read, on every head unit.

  **Affected trips are repaired once, automatically** (and again after a backup is restored): the starting value is replaced by the trip's first real reading, a few seconds later, and the trip's figures are recalculated. On one car seven trips were affected — all of them the first drive of a morning — and a 10 km commute that showed −16,774,354 kWh comes back as 3.8 kWh.

- **Charging sessions that never happened: small "Reconstructed" charges of about 1 kWh after a drive** — when the car wakes, the app compares the battery level with the one it last saw, so a charge that happened while it wasn't running still gets recorded. It compared the **BMS** reading, which sags further than the dashboard's percentage while you drive and creeps back up once the car rests — 1 to 2 % over the following hours. That rebound was taken for a charge: a car left in an underground garage for four and a half hours came back with a "Reconstructed" session of 1.22 kWh, the dashboard percentage unchanged at 50 % → 50 %. A real charge moves the dashboard percentage too, so a small BMS rise with the dashboard figure unchanged is now recognised as a rebound and not recorded (`diag.log` notes each one: `charge reconstruction skipped … rest rebound`). A rise of 3 % or more is still trusted on its own, so an off-state charge isn't missed if the dashboard figure is slow to update when the car wakes.

- **DiLink-5: charging was never detected** — the app reads the car's charging power on DiLink-5, but stored it without the timestamp that marks a reading as current, so it never counted as a charge: **no charging session was recorded**, and the dashboard never showed the car charging. A trip still open when the car was plugged in stayed open through the charge — on one Sealion 7 a 35-minute DC charge became part of the drive before it, until the 30-minute safety net closed the trip, with its dashboard percentage going from 16 % to 74 % within one "trip". Charging is now recognised from two readings together, because power alone can't tell: with no cable plugged in, DiLink-5 reports a fixed **359.4 kW**. A charge counts when the car reports a charging cable plugged in **and** a power above zero and up to 400 kW, other than that fixed value — the readings measured on a Sealion 7 through one DC charge, which ramped from 5.6 to 107 kW. A single odd reading mid-charge doesn't end the session; twelve seconds without one does. A charge that starts during a trip also ends that trip's standstill hold, so the trip closes after the usual auto-stop time instead of running on through the charge. `diag.log` notes the cable going in and out and each charge starting and ending (`🔌 DiLink-5 …`). DC charging is the case measured so far; a slow AC charge is the next to confirm. DiLink-3 is untouched.

- **DiLink-5: the dashboard showed "0.0 (9.9)" after a drive** — opening the app during or after a trip showed the Distance card's *This session* figure as 0.0 beside the trip total, and it stayed at 0.0 until the car moved again. The app places the start of the current session using the car's own trip meter, which DiLink-5 doesn't have, and without one it placed it wherever the car happened to be standing. It now falls back to the start of the trip: with no long stop in between, the session *is* the trip, so the distance shows as a single figure.

- **The first battery reading after the car wakes is no longer thrown away** — the filter that ignores a one-off jump in the battery system's state of charge could take the 0 it reports while starting up as its reference, and then ignore the first real reading as a jump from 0. A 0 reference now counts as no reference at all. Minor — the following reading always got through — but `diag.log` filled with lines like `held 0.0, ignored 82.0`.

- **`diag.log` reported a speed reading as overdue when none had ever arrived** — the speed-stall line says how long ago the car last sent a raw speed event, counted from a figure that starts at zero, so a car that has never sent one looked exactly like a car whose last one came at boot: the number simply tracked how long the head unit had been switched on. It also printed the same *none* for the scale it works out from those events whether it was still working it out or would never have anything to work from. On DiLink-5, where that event has never been seen to fire at all, telling those two apart took counting across 14,406 lines of log. The line now says **`spEvent=never-arrived`** outright, and *n/a* rather than *none* for the scale. Diagnostics only: nothing about how speed is measured changed, and on those cars it has always come from a different route. Reported by **[@luads](https://github.com/luads)**, from 241 stall samples across two boots on a Sealion 7 in which the event had not fired once.

- **An app update could never replace the bundled Tailscale daemon** — the app installed it once and then skipped the step whenever a daemon was already running; and even without that skip the copy would have failed, because the target is a running executable and Linux refuses to write over one. A car therefore kept running whichever build it first installed, so 2.17.0's new HTTPS support came back as *"unknown subcommand: serve"* from a daemon that predated the feature. The daemon is now hash-checked on every start, staged and **renamed** into place (a rename, unlike a copy, leaves the running process alone), and restarted only when it genuinely changed.

- **An installed web companion could stay on an older version after an update** — once the companion is reached over **HTTPS**, which is new in this release, the browser keeps a copy of it on the phone so it can still open when the car can't be reached. That copy was served first, ahead of the car, so a companion added to the home screen could keep showing the version it was first installed with, however many times the app updated. It now always loads from the car, and falls back to the stored copy only when the car doesn't answer within three seconds. The stored copy is renewed by stamping each app version into it, and the build skipped that step whenever the version number was the only thing that had changed — one beta went out still stamped with the previous version. The stamp is now applied on every version change. A companion opened over plain `http://` never keeps a copy, so it was never affected.

- **A scheduled Telegram backup could arrive as a settings file with no database behind it** — settings travel with every backup since **2.16.0**, and on the automatic schedule they were sent *first*, deliberately: a database too large for Telegram's **50 MB** limit is skipped, and sending the small file ahead of that check meant at least something got out. It reads as the exact opposite of what happened. The weekly message arrives, it holds a **settings** file, and the trip database it belongs to never left the car — a backup that looks like it happened. On a database that has grown past the limit, which is precisely when it matters, that is a silent gap dressed up as a delivery. The settings file is now sent **after** the size check, so the two either arrive together or not at all, and an over-limit database produces the same "nothing sent" outcome it did before 2.16.0. **Send Backup Now** follows the same rule. Settings can still be exported whenever you like from **Backup & Restore → Settings backup & restore**, which writes them to the Download folder however large the database has become.

## [2.16.0] - 2026-Sep-12

> **What's new in a nutshell**
>
> One new feature, two fixes, one discovery, and the next step in the ongoing **DiLink-5 auto-start** investigation. The feature is that **backups now carry your settings as well as your trips**: a backup was the database and nothing else, so a reinstall restored every trip and none of the **tariff, goals, ABRP/MQTT connections, layout or language** around it. Every backup now writes a small settings file beside the database, wherever that backup goes, and restoring it is a ticked-by-default choice on the restore dialog — with a switch deciding whether it carries your passwords and tokens. The first fix applies to anyone who installed the app since **2.13.0**: a brand-new installation never saw the one-time authorisation prompt its background helper needs unless the car was a DiLink-5, so **instant speed, gear and power** never worked and nothing in the app could put it right — existing installations were unaffected and had no way to notice. The app will ask for that authorisation the next time you open it. **MQTT over WebSocket** now works too: turning on **Use WebSocket** previously failed the moment the connection was established, ruling out reaching a self-hosted broker through a **reverse proxy or a Cloudflare Tunnel** — thanks to **[@boraita](https://github.com/boraita)** for the diagnosis and for verifying the fix on a real car. The discovery is that the **Atto 3 EVO** ships a head unit newer than any the app had met — **DiLink 100**, on Android 14 — and that the app cannot currently read battery, range, odometer, temperatures or charging on it, though **speed, gear, power and trip distance do work**. The cause is still being pinned down and no fix is claimed here; what this release adds is the diagnostic detail needed to find it. On **DiLink-5**, auto-start is **still not solved**, but the helper now starts the moment its channel appears instead of at guessed intervals, recovery after the car force-stops the app drops from ninety seconds to ten, and `diag.log` records enough to tell the remaining failure modes apart. **DiLink-3 keeps its existing start-up path** — the authorisation prompt above is what changes for it.

### Added

- **Your settings are now backed up with the database** — a backup has always been the trip database and nothing else, so a reinstall restored every trip and none of the configuration around them. The selected car, units and theme, the dashboard layout and card order, the **electricity tariff and currency**, **personal goals**, the **MQTT**, **ABRP** and **Telegram** connections, the web companion port and PIN, the language, the tyre-pressure unit and the **Pro unlock code** all live in the app's preferences, which an uninstall wipes — and nothing in a `.db` file could bring them back. Every backup now writes a second file beside the database, `byd_stats_settings_v<version>_<date>.json`, a couple of kilobytes of plain text, to exactly the same places: **Download**, the private ADB directory, the **SD card**, and your **Telegram chat** — including on the automatic daily/weekly/monthly schedule, where it is sent *before* the database, so that a database too large for Telegram's 50 MB limit no longer means nothing at all gets out that week.

  The two files share a timestamp, and that is how they are paired. Restore a database whose settings file is still present and the confirmation dialog offers **Also restore the settings saved with this backup**, ticked by default and one tap to untick — restoring an old database onto a working installation should not silently replace your current tariff and broker password along with it. Restoring settings **never touches trips or charging sessions**, and the app does **not** close to do it: every value goes back through the same code that saves it when you change it by hand, so it is live immediately. A change of **language** is the single exception, and only restarts the screen.

  Nothing is asked of anyone who never reinstalls — the file is simply written and ignored. Backups made by earlier versions have no settings file, and restore exactly as they always did.
- **Settings can be backed up and restored on their own** — a new **Settings backup & restore** card sits at the bottom of *Settings → App → Backup & Restore*, under the database sections. It lists every settings file the app can find, newest first, each with **Restore** and **Delete**, and adds a **Back up settings now** button for when you have just changed a tariff and would rather not wait for the next database backup. After a reinstall the file is normally still sitting in `Download/BydTripStats/` — that folder survives an uninstall — so it can be restored before the database, after it, or instead of it.

  The card also carries the **Include credentials** switch, **on** by default. With it on, the file holds the **MQTT password**, the **ABRP** and **Telegram** tokens, the **web companion PIN** and your **Pro code**, because a connection restored without its password comes back configured and dead. That file lives in your own car's Download folder, beside your own trip database, which is why it defaults to on. Turn it off before sharing one — to copy a configuration to a second car, or to attach it to a bug report — and everything else is still exported; restoring such a file fills in all the rest and leaves your existing passwords untouched rather than blanking them. Your **Pro code** is safe to carry either way: it is re-checked against the vehicle it was issued for on every launch, so a copied code unlocks nothing on anyone else's car.

  One limit worth stating plainly: the settings file is *sent* to Telegram with every backup so an off-car copy exists, but the **Telegram restore list still offers databases only**. Settings are restored from the local list described above. Full details in **BACKUP.md**.
- **`diag.log` now shows whether BYD's auto-start toggle has the app switched off** — the channel diagnosis line gains an `rcv=` field reporting the state of the app's own start-up receiver, which is the exact thing BYD's **auto-start** settings screen switches on and off. `rcv=OFF` means no start-up signal will ever be delivered, no matter what else is healthy — a completely different problem from the car having force-stopped the app, yet until now the two looked identical in a log and could only be told apart by asking the owner to go and check the screen. Read from the app's own component, so it needs no permission and no working channel — which matters, because the channel may itself be what's broken.
- **Full retry detail is kept through the boot window in `diag.log`** — repeated identical lines are normally collapsed into a periodic heartbeat so that a day of 15-minute retries can't shred the file's history. During the first twenty minutes after a head-unit boot that collapsing is suspended (hard-capped at 25 lines, after which the heartbeat resumes as before), because in that window the retries *are* the evidence. A cold boot previously left a single "channel closed" line followed by a nine-hour silence, which reads exactly like an app that never retried at all — the one thing the log most needed to rule out.

- **The vehicle compatibility report now identifies the platform it came from** — a report said which car the owner had selected but nothing about the head unit running the app, so an unexpected one had to be worked out by asking its owner to run commands over adb. It now opens with the **installed build**, the head unit's own **platform identifier** and **Android version**, and a **line per BYD permission** showing which are granted, which are refused, and which the head unit does not define at all — the last of those explaining, on the first DiLink 100 report, why several vehicle devices never appeared. The table is only meaningful against a baseline from cars that work, which is exactly why it is now captured on every car instead of being reconstructed by hand on the one that went wrong. Diagnostics only; nothing changes on cars that already work.

### Changed

- **The app now leaves a way back when it closes itself** — restoring, resetting and trimming the database all have to close the app so the database reopens cleanly, and the app then asks Android to start it again a moment later. That request is an activity start from a process that no longer exists, which the system only accepts within a few seconds of the app's last visible window — and the alarm carrying it was an *inexact* one, free to be held back past exactly that window, after which it is refused silently with nothing in the app able to tell. The alarm is now scheduled **exactly**, and a silent **Tap to reopen BYD Trip Stats** notification is posted before the app closes, in every case: it is the way back on head units that deliberately do not relaunch themselves, and the safety net everywhere else. Where the relaunch does work the notification is cleared the moment the app is up, about a second later, so nothing is left sitting in the shade.

  Alongside it, **which head units relaunch themselves** is now one decision rather than four separate copies of it, and it follows the same "newer than DiLink-3 is unfamiliar, so treat it as unsafe" rule as the rest of this release — a self-restart races the SDK injection there. **DiLink-3 is unchanged** and still reopens by itself; **DiLink-5** and **DiLink-100** now say so in the confirmation message instead of promising a restart that was never going to come, and get the notification instead.
- **DiLink-100: the app no longer installs its own updates** — in-app updating downloads the new APK and hands it to Android's silent installer, then restarts the app. That has been switched off on **DiLink-5** since **2.13.0**, where the head unit is unprivileged (the install can't complete anyway) and the post-install restart can wedge it; those owners get the update notice with `adb install -r` instructions instead. The check asked *"is this DiLink-5?"* — a match on the unit's own product string — so a **DiLink-100** was neither DiLink-5 nor recognised as anything else, and kept driving the silent installer and the restart after it. It now takes the same manual path as DiLink-5. This is precaution rather than a fix for an observed fault, and it is the same rule the compatibility probe, disk-reclaim and self-restart already follow in this release: **anything newer than DiLink-3 is unfamiliar and is treated as unsafe**. **Update checking is unchanged everywhere** — the badge and the "update available" notice still appear on every car, and **DiLink-3 still updates itself in place** exactly as before.
- **DiLink-5: the background helper is now started when the local channel appears, instead of at guessed intervals** — these units reboot themselves overnight, and the app starts very early in that boot, around eleven seconds in, when the channel the helper needs does not yet exist. The old approach tried at four fixed moments (immediately, then 30 s, 60 s and 3½ minutes later) and could spend every one of them against a channel that was still closed; if it opened shortly afterwards, nothing was left to notice. The app now **watches for the channel and starts the helper the instant it appears**, checking every 5 seconds for the first two minutes and every 15 seconds after that, for up to twenty minutes from boot — a ceiling chosen because a car left parked has powered itself off by then. The watch is deliberately cheap: a check against a closed channel is refused in microseconds, so waiting costs nothing until there is actually something to do, and it adds **no wake-ups** — the process is already running throughout, and an off car stays dark exactly as before. It also stops early if the app doesn't have the permissions the helper needs, rather than watching for twenty minutes on a car that could never have used it.

  **This is not by itself a fix for DiLink-5 auto-start**, and it will not help every car. It assumes the channel eventually comes back on its own after a restart — and on at least one Sealion 7 it does not, requiring the owner to re-enable it by hand from the head unit's own service menu, something no app can do for itself. Where that is the case, the watch will simply find nothing and the new boot-window logging will show exactly that, which is what identifies the remaining obstacle rather than leaving "it didn't start again" unexplained. Investigation continues.

  **DiLink-3 is normally unaffected by this change**: its channel is already up at boot, so it takes the same first-attempt path it always did and never enters the watch.
- **Disk-reclaim now behaves the same on every head unit newer than DiLink-3** — the last step of **Trim database** (VACUUM, which shrinks the file after rows have been removed) has to close the database and restart the app to finish, and that step has been switched off on **DiLink-5** since **2.13.0**, where an app-initiated restart can wedge a system service badly enough to leave the head unit looping. The check asked *"is this DiLink-5?"* and treated everything else as safe, so a **DiLink 100** head unit ran the restart with nothing guarding it; it now covers anything newer than DiLink-3. This is precaution rather than a fix for an observed fault — the particular failure seen on DiLink-5 cannot happen on that head unit — but an unfamiliar platform should be treated as unsafe rather than assumed safe. On the affected cars **Trim database** still removes every row it always did, so the database stops growing; only the final shrink is skipped, so the file doesn't get smaller, and the app no longer closes itself when the trim finishes. **DiLink-3 is unchanged** and still reclaims space exactly as before.
- **DiLink-5: quicker recovery after the car force-stops the app at ignition-off** — the background helper waited **90 seconds** after the app disappeared before waking it, a delay meant for *ambiguous* disappearances (a crash, a low-memory trim, or the app going deliberately dark in Deep Sleep) where waking instantly would fight something that might recover on its own. A force-stop is none of those, and on DiLink-5 it is what the car does at **every single ignition-off**. The helper now distinguishes the two — checked once when the app goes down, never repeatedly — and waits **10 seconds** in the force-stop case. Besides restoring recording sooner, this matters for the following morning: Android *persists* the force-stopped state, and an app still in it when the head unit powers down receives no start-up signal at the next boot, which is a state nothing on the car can escape unaided. The car allows roughly ten minutes between the force-stop and powering down, and the old delay was spending a meaningful part of that window for no benefit. Anything other than a confirmed force-stop keeps the original 90-second grace.

### Fixed

- **A new installation could be left permanently without its background helper** — the one-time authorisation prompt that grants the app the permissions its background helper needs stopped appearing on **any car not identified as DiLink-5**, from **2.13.0** onwards. The helper is what supplies **instant speed, gear and power**, and it refuses to start until that authorisation exists, so a fresh install on an affected car recorded trips without any of it and had **no way to reach the prompt at all** — it could only be fixed with a computer and via adb. Existing installations were unaffected and could not tell, because the authorisation is remembered once granted, which is why this went unnoticed for six weeks: the prompt had been tied to the DiLink-5 work it was introduced alongside, on the reasoning that DiLink-3 didn't need it, when in fact the helper had depended on it. The prompt is now shown on **every** head unit again, exactly as before 2.13.0. The DiLink-5-only part — the one system setting that release also introduced — stays DiLink-5-only and is untouched on other cars: nothing device-wide is changed anywhere else. If you installed the app fresh since 2.13.0 and have never seen instant speed, this release will ask for that authorisation the next time you open it.
- **The compatibility probe could send commands to an unrecognised head unit** — with **Vehicle compatibility** switched on, the probe reads every value each vehicle device offers, and on a device it doesn't recognise it cannot tell a harmless reading from an instruction. It has been held back from doing this on DiLink-5 since **2.13.0**, where it could wedge a system service badly enough to leave the head unit restarting in a loop; that guard, however, asked *"is this DiLink-5?"* and treated everything else as DiLink-3, which is safe — so a **DiLink 100** head unit, a generation newer again, was swept in full, reaching genuine commands including **display reset**, **module wake** and **software-update start**. The guard now asks whether the unit is **newer than DiLink-3**, so an unfamiliar platform is treated as unsafe rather than assumed safe. DiLink-3, the only generation this was ever verified harmless on, is unchanged and still captures a full report. A second reporting error is fixed alongside it: refused readings are large negative error codes, and the report's summary counted them as real values, telling us tyre pressures were being read successfully on a car where every single reading had failed.
- **MQTT over WebSocket failed immediately after the connection was established** — turning on **Use WebSocket** (**Settings → Connections → MQTT**), with or without TLS, dropped the connection the moment the handshake completed, so nothing ever reached the broker and *Test & Save* reported a failed publish. The MQTT library treats WebSocket support as optional and doesn't bring in the piece that implements it, so those classes were missing from the app entirely; a build-time rule was also hiding the fact that they were absent, which is why this shipped looking healthy and only failed on the car. The missing component is now included, and the rule that concealed it removed, so a future recurrence fails the build instead of the car. This affects **only** the WebSocket transport — plain MQTT and `mqtts` on a directly-exposed port were always fine — and it unblocks the common zero-port-forward setup of **Mosquitto behind a reverse proxy or a Cloudflare Tunnel**, which the README's "reachable from the internet" advice otherwise steers you toward. Reported, diagnosed and verified end-to-end on a Sealion 7 by **[@boraita](https://github.com/boraita)** ([#21](https://github.com/angoikon/byd-trip-stats/issues/21)).
- **About & FAQ reported the wrong Android version on every head unit except DiLink-3** — the *Platform* row was a fixed string reading **Android 10 · API 29**, which is DiLink-3's version and was printed unchanged on every car. A **DiLink-5** (Android 11, API 30) and a **DiLink-100** (Android 14, API 34) both claimed to be Android 10. It now reads the version from the head unit itself and appends the unit's own product identifier where it exposes one, so the row identifies the car it is actually running on.

> **What's new in a nutshell**
>
> A maintenance release. On **DiLink-5** cars set to the default **Panel** battery reading, **Trip History** showed the **same battery percentage at the start and at the finish** of every completed drive — a flat *"80% → 80%"* — even on journeys that had obviously used charge; open the same trip and its **Waypoints** and **SoC chart** told the truth all along, because only the trip's summary line was wrong, never the recorded data. ### Fixed

- **Trip History showed an identical start and finish battery % on DiLink-5 (a flat "80% → 80%", and a SoC change of 0.0%)** — visible with the **Panel** SoC source, which is the default; on the **BMS** source the same trips looked fine, which is why this went unreported for so long. When a trip is closed on the next app start rather than live (the normal case on DiLink-5, where the head unit force-stops the app at ignition-off), there was no live reading available and the trip's saved *finish* panel SoC fell back to its *start* value. Nothing else is recalculated: the battery percentages are display values, and no distance, energy or cost figure derives from them.
- **A stale 0% could be stored as a trip's finishing BMS SoC** — the **BMS**-source half of the same close-path hole: on that next-start path a final sample taken while the battery system was offline (which reads 0) was written straight through as the trip's end SoC, giving history an "80% → 0%". A zero is now recognised as a missing reading, and the lowest genuine SoC recorded during the trip is used instead.

Two trips count as the same route when their **start and end locations match** (within ~250 m) **and** their distances are similar — and direction matters, so your commute **to** work and the drive **home** are tracked as separate routes (their efficiency genuinely differs with elevation, traffic and time of day). All computed on-device from coordinates the app already records; no new data is collected.
- **Trip tagging** — label trips with reusable, **auto-coloured** custom tags (e.g. "commute", "motorway", "errand"). Add or remove tags on a trip's **detail screen**, or **bulk-tag** several trips at once from History's selection mode (alongside Merge / Compare / Delete); tags appear as coloured chips in the History list. **Filter** the history list by one or more tags from the filter sheet, and a dedicated **Tags** screen rolls up each tag's trip count, total distance, total energy and average efficiency — so you can compare categories like "motorway" vs "city" at a glance — with options to rename or delete a tag, or jump straight to its trips. Tags are created once and reused; deleting a tag keeps the trips, and merging two trips combines their tags.
- **Send the vehicle-compatibility probe report by QR code** — sharing a compatibility report no longer needs Telegram, adb, or fishing a saved file off the head unit. Settings → *Vehicle Compatibility* gains an **Email via QR code** button: it uploads the report to a temporary public host (litterbox — auto-deleted after **12 hours**) and shows a **QR code** that opens a pre-filled email to bydtripstats@gmail.com with the one-tap download link already in it. Scan it with your phone, pick your email account and press Send — the head unit has no mail app, so this hands the report off to the device that does. A **Copy link** fallback and the raw URL are shown too, and the privacy notice now spells out that this option uploads the report to a temporary public host. The existing **Telegram** and **Save** (to Download) options are unchanged.
- **MQTT over WebSocket (ws / wss), plus an explicit TLS toggle** — the MQTT publisher previously connected only over raw TCP, which can't be routed through an HTTP reverse proxy. You can now enable **WebSocket** transport (with a configurable path, default `/mqtt`) and **TLS** independently in Settings → Connections → MQTT, so users running their own broker on a home server can expose it through nginx / Traefik / Caddy as `wss://your-host/mqtt` instead of opening a raw MQTT port. The broker must have a WebSocket listener enabled (e.g. Mosquitto's `protocol websockets`); the reverse proxy forwards to it. Plain-TCP setups are unaffected — both toggles default off, and existing TLS-on-8883 configs keep working.

### Fixed

- **A DC fast charge started while the car was switched on wasn't recorded as a charging session** — charging detection leaned on the car's *pushed* BMS charging signals (charge-gun / charger-work state, delivered-capacity), and on this firmware that channel can stall: a DC charge begun with the head unit fully powered left them silent, so the dashboard showed the live charge but no session opened. The app now reads the car's own **charge-power meter** (battery voltage × current) directly — a reading it previously logged but never used, and which the old power path couldn't see because it was capped around the 50 kW AC range — so the charge is both detected and recorded with its true power curve (instead of the 0 kW stored before). As a backstop it also treats a large, sustained power flowing *into* the pack while parked as charging. **AC charging is untouched**, and it's pure-EV only — so a plug-in hybrid charging its pack from the engine in Park is never mistaken for a plug-in charge. Charging while the car is **off** is unchanged — still reconstructed from the State-of-Charge gain on the next wake-up.
- **MQTT entities could get stuck "unavailable" in Home Assistant even while telemetry kept publishing** — the publisher announced itself `online` only once, at connect, and relied on the Last Will to mark it `offline` on an unexpected drop. If a stray `offline` ever landed while the app was actually still connected and publishing — for example a retained will arriving after the broker handed the client ID to a fresh connection — nothing ever flipped availability back to `online`, so Home Assistant showed the device unavailable even though fresh state was arriving on the same broker. The app now re-asserts the retained `online` availability periodically (at most once every 30 s, so it adds no meaningful traffic to the live stream), letting availability self-heal within one cycle. Seen with self-hosted brokers; brokers on a always-reachable host rarely produced the stray `offline` in the first place.
- **WiFi SSID was always empty (blank in Home Assistant and in Settings)** — the `wifi_ssid` field was wired through to MQTT and the Settings "Wi-Fi" row, but the value was never actually read — the telemetry builder hardcoded it to an empty string, so the MQTT payload omitted it entirely. The app now reads the connected network name from the head unit (cached, refreshed at most every 30 s) and publishes it. Note: Android only exposes the SSID when location permission is granted **and** location is switched on; with location off the system reports it as unknown, so the field stays blank by design.
- **Charging "Temperature" chart was squashed, with the Cell min / Cell max lines pinned to 0 °C early in a session** — some BYD battery firmwares report only the *average* cell temperature at first and don't expose the per-cell minimum/maximum until partway through a charge. The chart drew those not-yet-reported readings as 0 °C, which both anchored the two lines to the bottom and pulled the whole temperature axis down to 0 — squashing the real ≈30–40 °C range into a sliver at the top. Each line now starts where its data actually begins, and the axis scales to the temperatures present, so the variation is readable. The average line is unchanged — it was available throughout.

- **Switching to Minimal while already parked never caught off-state charging** — the off-state keepalive (which wakes the app to sample overnight charging in Minimal) was only (re)armed on a car off→on→off cycle, not when you picked the mode in Settings. Coming from Deep Sleep — which cancels that keepalive — and switching to Minimal without driving left no keepalive scheduled at all, so plugging in the charger produced no telemetry. Selecting a mode now immediately reconciles background scheduling (arm the keepalive for Minimal, cancel it and go resident for Always On, cancel it for Deep Sleep). Valid between beta versions - released won't have this issue.
- **Wrong resume metrics after engine off/on within a trip** (Minimal/Deep Sleep) — the trip now re-syncs from the database on resume so distance, energy, and consumption stay continuous.
- **Live segment distance frozen at "0.0" after a brief stop and restart** — the live Distance was computed purely from the odometer, which the car's mileage ECU reports late for several seconds after an engine restart, so the new segment stayed at 0 even while moving. The speed-integrated distance (which was being calculated but never shown) now bridges that gap, so Distance keeps moving the instant any speed reading is available and snaps back to the odometer once it ticks.
- **Speed frozen at 0 while driving (poll-only getter going stale)** — the dashboard speed came solely from polling the BYD `getCurrentSpeed` getter, which on some firmwares freezes at a stale 0 after an auto-started trip and only recovers on an engine off/on. The speed device's live **event stream** (the same mechanism that keeps motor RPM updating) was being received but only logged, never applied. Speed is now driven from that event stream — the app auto-learns the event-to-km/h scale while the getter still works, then keeps the speedometer alive from events when the getter stalls, falling back to the getter when events aren't flowing.
- **Gear stuck on "P" while driving** — on firmwares where the gearbox device never reports a gear (its `gearCode`/`currentGear` getters return NULL and no gear event fires), the gear stayed pinned to its default "P" even at speed, because the code trusted the gearbox source unconditionally and never fell back to the speed-based guess. Since gear=D/R is also used as a "car on" proxy, this could knock other readings off too. A physical-impossibility guard now overrides "P" to "D" whenever the car is clearly moving forward (>5 km/h), and a diagnostic was added to pinpoint whether the gearbox device is silent or actively misreporting.
- **Speed flickering to 0 mid-drive from the event stream overriding a healthy getter** — on firmwares where the speed event's raw value isn't proportional to km/h, the learned scale was meaningless and the event-derived value (which could land anywhere, including ~0) was overriding a perfectly good `getCurrentSpeed` reading whenever an event had arrived in the last 3 s — i.e. constantly while driving. The polled getter is now authoritative whenever it's healthy; the event stream only bridges a genuinely frozen getter, and only when GPS or traction power independently confirm the car is moving (so it can't invent speed at a standstill either).
- **Energy consumption over-reported (regen not credited)** — the per-tick energy accumulator summed only *positive* changes in the BYD consumption counter, which silently discarded every regen tick and reported **gross** energy instead of **net**. On trips with meaningful regen (e.g. a long descent) this over-stated consumption substantially. The accumulator now sums both signs (discharge minus regen) for true net consumption, while still dropping the counter's car-on reset so multi-segment trips stay correct. The stored trip energy also no longer prefers the running *peak* discharge (which sits above the end value once you regen past it).

### Fixed

- **Trip energy under-reported by ~half on cars whose BMS counter resets at engine-on** — the saved `kWh` for a trip is now the sum of positive per-tick changes in the BMS discharge counter, not the simple end-minus-start of the counter. On firmwares that reset `getTotalElecConValue` to ~0 at every engine-on cycle (notably the Seal Excellence AWD), a single trip that spanned an engine-off pause was previously capturing only one segment's worth of consumption — typically around half. The same fix also feeds the live "energy consumed" display on the dashboard so the running number matches what the trip will save. EVs with a lifetime-cumulative counter are unaffected — the sum is mathematically identical to end-minus-start when no reset happens.
- **State of charge reading 0% on some plug-in hybrids** — SoC is now read from BYD's standard `getElecPercentageValue` / `getSOCBatteryPercentage` getters when the internal getter mapping returns nothing (or a zero), instead of failing silently. Fully-charged EVs are unaffected — the fallback only engages when the primary path yields no usable reading. A second guard now also rejects bad SoC values arriving via the SDK's event channel (null / sentinel / out-of-range), which would previously overwrite a good polled value and snap the dashboard to 0.
- **Regen mode indicator showing the wrong level** — the indicator was driven by the powertrain energy mode (EV / Forced-EV / HEV), so switching to Forced-EV wrongly showed "Higher" regen and EV showed "Standard". Regen level is now sourced only from the genuine regen signal.
- **Regen indicator on cars that don't report a regen level** — the Driving Dynamics card no longer shows "UNKNOWN" or nags to "change regen modes" on firmwares (notably DM-i PHEVs) that never expose a regen level; it shows only the drive mode in that case.
- **Triple-digit "AVG" briefly shown when recording is started manually mid-drive** — pressing Record while already moving (auto recording off) could flash an average speed of several hundred km/h for a few seconds before the trip-anchored values took over. The trip-controls card now resets distance / time / segment immediately at the press moment so the AVG starts from zero and climbs to the real value as the trip accumulates, instead of inheriting the pre-trip back-anchored journey distance divided by a brand-new elapsed timer.
- **Charging session stuck "in progress" on plug-in hybrids — including after a fresh install** — on some DM-i firmwares the BMS never returns its `chargerWorkState` to idle after charging finishes. The existing ten-minute stale-state safety net previously only fired if capacity activity had been observed in the current session, so a fresh install connecting to a car with a stuck work state would never trigger it. The guard now also activates based on how long `chargerWorkState` has been non-zero without any corresponding power or capacity data, covering the fresh-install case.
- **PM2.5 showing `0` on cars without an interior air-quality sensor** — vehicles that don't have a PM2.5 sensor return `0` from the SDK, which was previously passed through and displayed as `0/— μg/m³`. The reading is now suppressed for values of `0` since a genuine PM2.5 level of zero is not physically measurable by in-cabin sensors; those cars will show no PM2.5 subtitle on the Environment card instead.
- **PM2.5 not updating after initial connection on some PHEVs** — PM2.5 was read once at climate-device registration but never again; on PHEV firmware the debounce requires two consecutive identical readings to confirm a value, so the single initial read left the reading permanently pending. PM2.5 is now re-polled from the climate device every 5 seconds so the debounce can confirm.
- **Range metric showing 0 or the fuel driving range on PHEVs** — the range projection used `soc` (BMS) to compute remaining energy; when BMS SoC briefly reads 0 at engine wake-up the projection collapsed to 0 or, on PHEVs, snapped to the fuel-only range (e.g. 900 km). SoC now falls back to the panel reading when BMS reports 0. Additionally, the Range stat card on PHEVs now shows the car's own EV-only range estimate instead of the trip projection (which included the fuel offset and produced misleadingly large numbers).
- **Mud / Sand drive-mode labels were swapped** — corrected to match BYD's mode constants.

### Fixed

- **Live "energy consumed" no longer spikes to thousands of kWh at trip start** — and now stays in step with the trip overview's stored value (which is what reference apps like Electro report for the same drive). The live Trip Tracking card reads the BMS `totalDischarge` counter delta against the *corrected* trip-start anchor exposed by `TripRepository.currentTripStartTotalDischarge()`. The repository runs a per-tick self-correction (see [TripRepository.kt](app/src/main/java/com/byd/tripstats/data/repository/TripRepository.kt)) the moment the BMS produces a plausible reading after a stale `0` at trip start, so the live delta picks up the corrected anchor automatically instead of carrying the multi-thousand-kWh spike forward. As defence-in-depth for the brief window before the correction fires (or if the override path supplies an unsane override), the displayed value is clamped to the physical envelope `[0, 2 × pack capacity]` and falls back to the power-integrated traction estimate outside it.
- **Range projection now reports a realistic value from the first kilometre instead of being pinned in `● Baseline`** — on Seal Excellence (and any car with a sparse or unreliable engine-power signal) the projection used to sit on the catalog baseline for the entire drive, sometimes underestimating range by half. Several things changed together: (a) the rolling-window energy accumulator and the speed-bin accumulator both feed from the BMS `totalDischarge` delta — the same source the CONS readout uses — with engine-power only as a fallback, both during a live drive and when restoring an in-progress trip from disk; (b) the speed-bin accumulator counts distance every tick, with energy added only when positive, so sparse BMS updates can't starve the bin distance below `BIN_MIN_DIST_KM`; (c) the `● Historical bins` tier averages Wh/km across *all* populated speed bins weighted by distance, and — crucially — falls back to the trip-cumulative consumption (the exact figure the CONS readout shows: trip-total kWh ÷ trip distance) whenever the speed bins themselves haven't accumulated usable energy. That trip-cumulative path always resolves once the drive has covered ≥ 0.2 km and consumed anything, so the projection leaves the catalog baseline regardless of how erratically the firmware increments its discharge counter; (d) the model badge is picked across all three tiers (`● Live trip` / `● Speed bins` / `● Baseline`) when the app is reopened mid-trip instead of skipping `● Speed bins` entirely; (e) the orange projection line is drawn from km 0 in the restored chart rather than leaving a 3 km gap before the old stabilisation threshold. End result on a typical city drive: the badge flips from `● Baseline` to `● Speed bins` within ~0.2 km, then to `● Live trip` at ~3 km, and the projection converges to roughly `(remaining kWh × 100) / CONS` from the first kilometre instead of after 30+ km of driving.
- **Range projection chart polish** — the projected orange line now starts at km 0 instead of waiting for the first stabilised point (was a small but visible gap near the y-axis); the y-axis stays well-formed on rare cold-boot ticks where the BMS hasn't reported a range yet at trip start (could previously degenerate to a zero-height chart); and saturated projections (above the WLTP cap, i.e. calibration not yet settled) now render as a soft asymptotic bulge into the top padding rather than a hard line clipped at the chart's ceiling, so they're visible regardless of starting SoC.
- **Live Distance metric now shows the new segment alongside cumulative trip distance after an engine-off resume** — e.g. `13.3 (17.8)` once you've driven 13.3 km in the post-resume segment of a 17.8 km trip. Previously the segment anchor was set from the BYD `currentJourneyDriveMileage` counter, which on some firmwares doesn't reset across a brief engine-off cycle — so segment ended up equal to cumulative and the parentheses never appeared. The off→on transition handler now anchors the segment at the current odometer directly, and the same correction is applied on app-restart restore by scanning the stored data points for the most recent off-state gap.
- **Range projection chart now colours each segment by its own projected-vs-BMS verdict** — the orange/green line and shaded area used to take a single colour based only on where the *current* projection sat relative to BMS, repainting the entire trip history in that colour. Each segment between consecutive sampled points is now classified independently: above the BMS line it renders green (you outperformed expectation across that stretch), below it renders amber, with the shaded area beneath the line matching segment by segment. The duplicate "Δ" label that used to float at the leading edge inside the chart is gone too — only the chip in the top-right of the chart frame and the existing vertical tape-measure line + tick caps remain as the delta indicator.
- **Stored trip energy is now resistant to a stale `totalDischarge` anchor** — `TripRepository` now (a) re-anchors a trip's `startTotalDischarge` on the fly the moment the BMS produces a reading that implies the original anchor was a stale zero (observed delta exceeds both 2 × pack capacity and the power-integrated estimate of energy actually drawn so far), back-computing the corrected anchor from the integrated draw, (b) clamps the final `(endTotalDischarge − startTotalDischarge)` to the power-integrated estimate at trip-close time if the reported delta is still physically impossible, and (c) applies the same plausibility clamp at each 30 s segment flush, using a per-segment slice of the trip's power-integrated draw as the fallback (a 30 s segment can't physically consume more than ~2 kWh; anything above 50% of pack capacity is unambiguously corrupt). Together these protect the stored `energyConsumed`, the per-segment `energyUsed`, and every downstream chart, consumption stat, monthly total, and seasonal analysis that depends on them against the same stale-zero failure mode that was corrupting the live display.
- **Battery temperature now shows a consistent, accurate value across all screens** — the Dashboard stat card, Trip Detail average, and Trip Detail min/max range now all agree with each other and reflect actual battery cell temperature (not a coolant-side probe that reads several °C lower).
- **Battery temperature decoder rewritten to match BYD's actual encoding** — earlier 2.4.1 builds tried to guess between two possible encodings (direct °C vs ÷3) using anchors and ambient as tiebreakers, and got it consistently wrong by a few degrees against reference apps (Electro, the open-source Overdrive). The decoder now uses BYD's documented `raw − 40 = °C` formula directly, with raw values validated to be in `0..120` (i.e. −40°C to 80°C). Result: cell temperatures match what every other BYD-aware app on your car reports, instead of being 4–5°C off in either direction.
- **Battery cell temp sentinels no longer clobber the last good reading** — the BMS-uninitialized value 0 and BYD's INVALID/UNAVAILABLE sentinels are filtered out so quiet polls don't overwrite a real temperature with a placeholder.
- **Old trips with bad stored temperature averages now display sensibly** — for trips recorded before this fix, the average battery temperature shown in Trip Detail is now re-derived from the min/max cell range rather than the previously stored (potentially wrong) average.
- **Battery temperature max is now shown in Trip Detail even when the car doesn't report it directly** — the max is estimated from the min and average, so you'll see a range like "20°C – 22°C" instead of just "20°C".
- **Trip export dialog sections are now collapsible** — "Save to Download folder" and "Send to Telegram bot" each have a tappable header with a chevron and start collapsed. The dialog now opens with just the Clipboard button and two section headers, so users can see the two groups exist at a glance and tap into whichever they want, instead of being faced with seven buttons up-front. The previously hidden third Telegram button ("Send HTML viewer to Telegram") is now discoverable inside the expanded section.
- **Trip duration and average speed no longer count engine-off resume time** — when you stop the engine and the trip stays open inside the configurable engine-off resume window (Settings → Preferences), the wallclock keeps ticking. Until now that time was getting added into the saved trip's duration, which made average speed read low (e.g. a 30 km trip you actually drove in 45 min would average ~20 km/h instead of ~40 km/h if the trip closed 45 min after the engine went off because the resume window was 30 min and you parked overnight). The stored duration and avg speed now reflect actual driving time only — gaps in the recorded data points where the car was off are subtracted. Existing trips are back-filled automatically on first launch after the upgrade (one-shot pass over every completed trip), and the live Trip Tracking card's TIME / AVG also subtract any mid-trip engine-off windows in real time. Schema bumped v3 → v4 to add the per-trip stored value.
- **Gear indicator no longer says "P / Trip in progress" when the car auto-parks at a stop** — BYD automatically shifts to P at a standstill (e.g. in traffic), which previously caused the gear circle to read "P" and the status to read "Trip in progress" — misleading, since the user hasn't actually parked. The gear circle now shows "·" in a muted colour and the status reads "Stopped" while a trip is active and the car has auto-shifted to P. A manual P (no active trip) still displays as "P" and "Waiting for Trip…".
- **Charging session no longer stays "in progress" after you unplug and drive away** — some firmwares don't fire the unplug callback when the plug is removed while the car is locked (most common: charge overnight, walk up in the morning, unplug, drive off). The plug-presence flag stayed stuck non-zero, so the app kept showing "charging" through the next drive and the overnight session in history stayed open. The "is charging now" signal now uses the BMS-level charger work state (which clears once the BMS actually finishes a session) as its primary input, with a defence-in-depth override that force-clears the charging state the moment the car is genuinely driving (speed > 5 km/h in D or R) — physically impossible to be charging at that point. On install, any session left open from before the fix is auto-closed on the next trip start with its end-of-data-points timestamp and final SoC.
- **MCU WiFi keepalive during charging now triggers on all three charging signals** — to prevent the car cutting WiFi ~15 minutes after ACC_OFF mid-charge, the periodic keepalive ping now fires whenever the BMS reports active charging, an active charger work state, *or* the plug being physically in the port. Earlier 2.4.1 builds used only the plug-presence signal; if the plug callback misfired, the keepalive missed and the app could lose telemetry mid-charge.
- **Telegram auto-backup no longer wastes data on oversized DBs** — the periodic Telegram backup worker (and any manual "Send to Telegram" tap) used to upload the entire database file before the Telegram Bot API rejected it with "Request Entity Too Large" — Telegram bots cap at 50 MB. A heavy user with a 100 MB DB on a daily schedule could push close to 500 MB of wasted traffic over five days. The worker now checks the DB size *before* the multipart upload starts, skips the run when the DB exceeds 50 MB, and surfaces a clear message ("Backup file is X MB — Telegram bots can only accept files up to 50 MB. Upload skipped to avoid wasting your data plan."). The local-backup path is unaffected.
- **ABRP uploads now pause when the car is parked and not charging** — previously the ABRP integration fired its upload loop from the foreground telemetry service whenever it was alive, which (with "Keep service alive when car is off" defaulting to true since 2.4.0) meant uploads continued 24/7 even though nothing meaningful about the car was changing. ABRP cares only about driving/charging telemetry, so the new gate (`!isCarOn && !isCharging` → skip) removes ~80% of the requests without losing any data that ABRP would actually use.
- **ABRP no longer reopens a fresh TLS connection on every upload** — `HttpURLConnection.disconnect()` was being called after every request, which forced the underlying TCP/TLS socket closed and disabled Android's HTTP connection-pool reuse. With ~17 k requests/day in the worst case, that was paying a ~5–8 KB TLS handshake on every single one. The success path now drains the response body and lets the pool keep the socket alive; disconnect still runs on the IOException path so a half-broken connection doesn't poison the pool. Saves ~9× bytes per request once the connection is warm.

## [2.4.0] - 2026-May-13

> **What's new in a nutshell**
>
> This release is primarily a reliability and polish update. The biggest change is that the app now stays connected by default while the car is off — this means the battery history chart no longer has gaps, and charging sessions that happen while you're away from the car are now captured properly (including real-time data points and correct kWh totals). UK and other Imperial users get a comprehensive fix pass: speed, distance, and consumption values are now correctly converted throughout all charts and screens, not just the summary cards. There's also a "Check for updates" button if you don't want to wait for the next app restart, and trip stats are displayed larger when the app is in full-screen landscape.

### Added

- **"Keep service alive when car is off" toggle** (Settings → Preferences, **default on**) — when enabled, the telemetry service no longer self-stops after the car has been off for 5 minutes. The benefits: the 48-hour HV/12V battery history chart receives continuous samples instead of long gaps; ADB-over-WiFi stays reachable without having to remote-wake the car first; trip-tracking responsiveness is unaffected by off-cycle restart latency. Real-world measurement since v2.3.0 shows the dominant overnight drain comes from stock BYD services (the GPS provider, Telenav ISA/ARP, and telematics heartbeat), not from BydTripStats. The v2.3.0 self-stop + off-state keepalive + periodic-restart-cancel code paths are all retained behind this toggle for users who specifically prefer the lower-churn behaviour.
- **Persistent diagnostic log** at `/sdcard/Android/data/com.byd.tripstats/files/diag.log` (caps at 256 KB + 256 KB rolled `.prev`). Captures BootReceiver actions, off-state keepalive schedule/fire/cancel, service onCreate/onDestroy/self-stop, and Application.onCreate with PID. Pullable any time via `adb pull` — useful for debugging service-lifecycle issues over windows much longer than the logcat ring buffer (which is only 256 KB on DiLink and gets shredded in ~7 minutes by the system GPS provider's 4 Hz wake-lock spam).

### Changed

- **Default off-state behaviour reverted to "always alive"** — see the toggle entry above. v2.3.0 introduced the self-stop based on the assumption that the app was a meaningful contributor to overnight HV drain. The uninstall test (drain rate identical with the app uninstalled) refuted that assumption. Continuous sampling is the more useful default; the self-stop remains available for users who want it.
- **Distance display and recorded trip distance now track the main odometer delta exclusively** — previously the live PowerMetric distance and stored trip distance were computed as `max(journeyMileage, odometerDelta, speedIntegration)`, which caused the app to show ~0.4 km more than the instrument cluster odometer over a 16 km trip. Both the live display and the finalised trip now use the SDK main odometer delta as the sole source of truth; `journeyMileage` and speed integration are retained only as fallbacks for the edge case where no odometer anchor is available (e.g. app connects mid-journey on a cold boot).

### Fixed

- **Off-state keepalive now armed defensively from the service itself** — previously the 90-minute off-state keepalive chain was scheduled only by `BootReceiver` on `ACC_OFF` / `ACC_MODE_CHANGED`. Investigation showed that the DiLink firmware does not reliably broadcast `ACC_OFF` on every car-off transition (e.g. remote-off, sleep timeouts), so the chain was never armed and the alarm fired zero times across 22 days of system uptime. The service now also schedules the keepalive directly from inside its self-stop path — by the time `carOff && !charging` for 5 minutes has been observed from real telemetry, we are independently certain the car is off and don't need a broadcast to confirm it. Only relevant when the new "keep service alive" toggle is disabled.
- **Range projection chart header now respects the selected unit system** — UK / Imperial users had a chart Y-axis correctly labelled "mi" while the header text underneath still read "447 km projected range" / "+196 km vs BMS estimate", and the WLTP saturation label said "≥ X km projected". The BMS-sourced figures already arrive in the car's native unit (UK BMSes report miles natively), so the values were correct; only the unit suffix needed to flip. The WLTP figure comes from the catalog (always metric) and is now properly converted to miles for Imperial display.
- **Imperial unit label fixes across multiple screens** — several consumption, distance and speed labels throughout the app (route analysis tab, trip charts, settings goals & personal bests summary) were still displaying metric suffixes regardless of the selected unit system. All affected values are now converted and labelled correctly for Imperial users.
- **Speed and Distance PowerMetric values now correctly converted for Imperial users** — the Speed tile was showing km/h values labelled "mph" (e.g. 32 instead of 20), and the Distance tile was showing km values labelled "mi" (e.g. 12.6 instead of 8). Both are now converted. The Range tile is unaffected — BMS-sourced range values already arrive in the car's native unit (miles for UK BMSes).
- **Live trip stats larger in full-screen landscape** — the TIME / AVG SPEED / ENERGY USED / CONSUMPTION readouts in the Trip Tracking card are rendered at a larger type scale when the app occupies the full screen, making them easier to read at a glance while driving.
- **Manual "Check for updates" button** (Settings → About & FAQ) — a "Check now" button has been added to the update status card so you can re-trigger the GitHub release check at any time, without waiting for the next app restart. The card shows a spinner and "Checking for updates…" while the request is in flight.
- **BYD Seal U DM-i Comfort** — 27.3 kWh Blade LFP (144S est.), 26.6 kWh usable, FWD 145 kW, 2,210 kg, 125 km EV-only WLTP.
- **BYD Seal U DM-i Design AWD** — 18.3 kWh Blade LFP (96S), 15.2 kWh usable, AWD (150 kW front + 120 kW rear), 2,100 kg, 70 km EV-only WLTP.
- **Motor card kW now displays correctly on cars that don't report RPM** — the kW subtitle in the right-panel motor card was gated on the RPM sensors being active; on BYD variants where `engineSpeedFront`/`engineSpeedRear` are not populated the card always showed "0 kW" even while driving. Power is now read directly from `enginePower` regardless of RPM availability, matching the behaviour of the left-panel Power metric.
- **Charging session reconstruction now fires on every car wake-up** — previously the SoC-delta reconstruction check (used to create a synthetic charging session for car-off charging periods) ran only once per *service lifetime*. With "Keep service alive when car is off" enabled, the service stays running through the entire off-state charge, so the reconstruction guard was already consumed when the car woke up and no session was created. The guard is now reset on every off→on transition, ensuring reconstruction is attempted every time the car wakes from a possible charging period.
- **Charging session reconstruction survives mid-reconstruction process restarts** — the pre-charge SoC baseline is now written to disk *after* reconstruction completes on the first car-on packet of each wake cycle, rather than immediately before the coroutine runs. Previously, if the process was killed between the baseline update and the DB insert (e.g. an app update installed right as the car woke up), the next start would read the already-updated post-charge SoC, see a delta of 0%, and silently skip reconstruction.
- **MCU WiFi keepalive now uses BMS-level charging gun state** — the 10-minute MCU keepalive (which prevents the car from cutting WiFi ~15 min after ACC_OFF) was previously only sent when the SDK explicitly reported `isCharging=true`. Direct power readings go stale in deep sleep even when charging is physically active, so the keepalive was never sent precisely when it mattered most. It now also triggers when the charging gun is physically present (`chargingGunState` from `AbsBYDAutoChargingListener`, a BMS-level callback that fires independently of ignition). This covers pre-charge negotiation, active charging, scheduled-charge wait, and post-charge while still plugged in — and clears automatically on unplug, so normal unconnected overnight parking is unaffected. `chargerWorkState` was evaluated and rejected as the keepalive signal: its `FINISH` and `TERMINATE` values are non-zero but indicate charging has already ended, which would have kept WiFi alive unnecessarily after a completed session.
- **Off-state charging now detected via charging gun presence** — the `isCharging` flag used by the telemetry loop to select the 30 s poll interval was previously derived solely from direct power readings, which require a fresh SDK event (< 4 s old) and go absent when the car is in deep sleep. The detection now also checks `chargingGunState` from `AbsBYDAutoChargingListener` — a physical signal that is non-zero whenever the gun is in the port, regardless of ignition or charging phase. This ensures the service correctly identifies an off-state charging session, switches to 30 s polling, and records real-time data points throughout rather than falling back to 5-minute ticks.

## [2.3.0] - 2026-May-10

### Added

- **Delete local backups from within the app** — each entry in the Local Backup list now has a delete button (trash icon). Tapping it shows a confirmation dialog then permanently removes the file from all locations (Downloads, internal ADB copy) in a single operation.
- **BYD Atto 2 Boost** — 51.1 kWh Blade LFP (100S estimated), FWD, 130 kW, 344 km WLTP.
- **BYD M6 Standard 120kW** — 55.4 kWh Blade LFP (102S), DC charging 89 kW.
- **BYD M6 Superior 150kW** — 71.8 kWh Blade LFP (132S), DC charging 115 kW. The existing M6 Superior entry has been renamed to "BYD M6 Superior 100kW" to distinguish the two; its stored ID is unchanged so existing car selections are preserved.
- **BYD Seal 6 Premium 95kW** — 56.64 kWh Blade LFP (118S, 150Ah × 377.6V), FWD, DC charging 103 kW, 425 km WLTC.
- **BYD Seal 6 Premium 160kW** — same 56.64 kWh pack (118S), RWD, 160 kW rear motor.
- **Distance shows no decimals in split-screen mode** — the Distance power metric no longer displays a digit after the decimal (e.g., "12.3") when the app resides in split-screen. Integer format is used to avoid crowding.
- **Added AUD (Australian Dollar) currency option** — the electricity cost tariff now supports A$ in addition to €, £, and $.

### Changed

- **Battery temperature removed from Battery stat card** — the value is unreliable across firmwares. Cars without a valid battery temp source (e.g. Seal Excellence) were displaying firmware sentinel values (186÷3=62°C, 195÷3=65°C, etc.) that could not be reliably distinguished from real readings without a stable ambient reference. The data continues to be recorded and displayed in the charging detail chart where it is more trustworthy.
- **rawJson no longer duplicates dedicated column data** — 25 fields already persisted in typed DB columns (soc, speed, gear, latitude, longitude, altitude, tyre pressures, tyre temperatures, cell voltages, battery total voltage, 12V voltage, soh, engine speeds, electric range, soc panel) have been removed from the per-point JSON blob. New data points are ~700 bytes smaller per row (~33% reduction). Fields that have no dedicated column (drive\_mode, regen\_mode, car\_on, statistic\_\*, instrument temps, bodywork diagnostics, etc.) are retained.

### Fixed

- **Battery cell temperature from statistic device auto-detects the encoding** — the BYD SDK returns `cellTAvg` / `cellTMin` / `cellTMax` in one of two encodings depending on firmware: direct °C on some firmwares, 1/3 °C units on others. Applying the wrong interpretation produced either an underread (e.g., 11.5°C when ambient was 32°C) or an overread (e.g., 62°C when ambient was 25°C). The fix uses the in-car ambient temperature as a reference and picks the interpretation whose deviation from ambient is physically plausible (within ~30°C parked/driving, ~50°C during DC fast charging).
- **Battery temperature sentinels suppressed when ambient is unavailable** — `resolveStatisticTempCelsius` was returning the divided value (raw ÷ 3) even when no ambient reference was available to validate it. Firmware sentinels like 186 (→ 186÷3=62°C) passed through undetected if the instrument device had not yet reported outdoor temperature. The function now returns null until ambient is known.
- **Service self-stop tripwire no longer fires on fresh start** — `lastFeatureEventElapsedMs` defaults to 0, which previously made the "SDK silent for 10 minutes" check evaluate to true immediately on first launch (any uptime exceeded the threshold before any SDK events arrived). The service would self-stop before SDK devices could register, blocking all telemetry until the user physically drove the car. The tripwire now requires at least one real SDK event before the silence threshold is evaluated.
- **Off-state idle no longer thrashes the service overnight** — three independent periodic restart sources (`ServiceWatchdogWorker` 15-min, `ServiceRestarterJobService` periodic 15-min, and `ServiceRestartReceiver` boot-action kicks) were re-starting the telemetry service throughout the night, undoing the carOff+notCharging self-stop and keeping the wake lock + WiFi lock held for ~33% of the overnight period. A new persistent `ServiceIdleState` flag is set when the service self-stops; every periodic restart source now reads the flag and skips the restart while idle. `BootReceiver` no longer re-arms restart kicks on `ACC_OFF` / `POWER_DISCONNECTED`. The off-state wake-lock duty cycle drops from ~33% to ~5%, eliminating the overnight 12V drain.
- **Service idle self-stop now truly stops the service** — the previous implementation called `stopSelf()` but did not set `intentionalStop = true`, causing `onDestroy` to immediately reschedule a restart via `scheduleSelfRestart`. The service was effectively cycling: stop → restart → 5 min → stop → restart, running all night and continuously publishing MQTT snapshots. Setting `intentionalStop = true` before `stopSelf()` breaks the loop; the service stays stopped until the next 90-minute alarm fires intentionally.
- **MQTT idle publish cadence corrected** — as a consequence of the above fix, MQTT now publishes a single snapshot every ~90 minutes while the car is off and idle (from the `OffStateKeepaliveReceiver` alarm), rather than every 5 minutes from the unintended restart loop. The MQTT settings hint has been updated to reflect this: "Idle: snapshot every ~90 min".
- **BYD SDK temperature sentinel values unconditionally rejected** — `resolveStatisticTempCelsius` now rejects raw values of 195 (`TEMPERATURE_INVALID`) and -60 (`TEMPERATURE_MIN`) before any ambient comparison logic. Previously these sentinel values could pass through when no ambient reference was yet available, producing spurious readings such as 65°C (195 ÷ 3) on the Seal Excellence firmware.
- **Live trip tracking card consumption matches finalized trip stats** — the energy counter in the trip tracking card now uses the battery discharge counter delta (`currentTotalDischarge − sessionStartTotalDischarge`), the same source used by finalized trip statistics. The previous motor power integration approach excluded AC, 12V auxiliary, and other non-motor loads, causing a systematic under-read of 10–20% when climate control was active.
- **Live trip tracking card average speed matches finalized trip stats** — average speed is now derived from the pure odometer delta (`currentOdometer − sessionStartOdometer`), matching the `endOdometer − startOdometer` formula used by finalized trips. The integration-based distance estimate is retained as a fallback only when the odometer has not yet advanced.
- **Backup deletion for "Download (file)" entries now works on Android 10+** — backups created by older app versions that wrote directly to the filesystem had no MediaStore record, so `File.delete()` and MediaStore-by-name lookups both silently failed. The deletion logic now follows a 4-step escalation: direct `File.delete()` → MediaStore lookup by file path (`DATA` column) → MediaStore lookup by display name → insert the file into MediaStore to gain ownership, then delete via the returned content URI.

### Maintenance

- **User-triggered "Trim database" action** in the Local Backup screen (new "Maintenance" section). Replaces the earlier auto-compaction-on-startup approach so the user runs it at their convenience and gets explicit feedback. Five phases run sequentially with live progress:
  - **A. Strip redundant rawJson fields** from every row in `trip_data_points` and `charging_data_points` (the 25 keys already persisted as typed columns).
  - **B. Clear rawJson** to `{}` for trip and charging points older than **45 days** — the diagnostic payload is no longer needed for historical data and recovers the largest amount of space.
  - **C. Downsample old trip points to 1 row per minute** for trips older than **60 days**. The native sampling rate of ~1 row per 30 s is preserved for recent trips; older trips keep `MIN(id)` per `(tripId, minute)` bucket. Distance, energy, and time-series shape remain accurate; chart smoothness is slightly reduced for very old trips.
  - **D. Delete charging data points for AC sessions older than 45 days.** AC vs DC is inferred from `charging_sessions.peakKw < 25 kW`; DC fast-charging sessions are kept entirely (rare and interesting to review). The session summary row (kWh added, peak kW, duration, etc.) is preserved for both.
  - **E. VACUUM via raw SQLite** — the telemetry service is briefly stopped, Room is closed, and `PRAGMA wal_checkpoint(TRUNCATE)` + `VACUUM` are run on a direct SQLite connection (the database file would otherwise stay locked by Room's connection pool, leaving freed pages unreclaimed). The app then auto-restarts so Room reopens cleanly — same pattern used after a database restore.
  - The result and timestamp are persisted across app restarts; the section header always shows "Last trimmed: …" with a one-line summary of the rows affected. Logged with tag `DatabaseTrimmer`. Expected to recover the bulk of the 500 MB+ accumulation since v2.1.0.

## [2.2.0] - 2026-May-08

### Added

- **Imperial / Metric unit system** — users can now choose between Metric (km, km/h, kWh/100km) and Imperial (mi, mph, kWh/100mi) in Settings → Preferences. The default is determined by the device locale (UK → Imperial, all others → Metric) and can be changed at any time with a two-button selector; the active choice is highlighted in the primary colour. The preference persists across restarts.
  - Odometer and speed values are telemetry-sourced and already arrive in the correct unit for the vehicle's market (BYD UK cars report miles and mph natively), so no conversion is applied to those — only the unit label changes.
  - Calculated distances (trip distance, monthly totals, range projection) and efficiencies (kWh/100km → kWh/100mi) are converted at display time; stored data always remains in metric.
  - The unit system is respected across the Dashboard, Trip History, Trip Detail, Trip Compare, Trip Goals, Seasonal Analysis, and all charts (Speed, Instant Consumption, Consumption history, Range Projection).
- **Live trip stats in the Trip Tracking card** — while a trip is being recorded, the card shows a real-time centre column: elapsed time (HH:MM:SS, continuous — does not pause during brief car-off stops within the 30-minute resume window), average speed, total energy used (kWh), and average consumption (kWh/100km or kWh/100mi). The card stays at a fixed height; the stats appear between the gear/status section on the left and the Auto toggle/Stop button on the right. All values respect the active unit system.
- **SoC overlay on the 12V / HV battery history chart** — the 48-hour battery monitoring dialog now overlays the HV State of Charge as a dashed purple line on a dedicated right-hand axis (0–100 %), making it easy to correlate 12V drain events with HV top-up cycles. SoC is sampled and persisted alongside the existing voltage readings. The dialog title was updated to "HV / 12V Batteries - Last 48 Hours" to reflect that both battery systems are now shown.
- **BYD Tang EV support** — the pure-electric Tang (AWD, 108.8 kWh Blade LFP, 530 km WLTP) is now available as a selectable BEV profile alongside the existing Tang DM-i.
- **In-app changelog viewer** — tapping "Changelog / What's new" in Settings → About now opens a scrollable in-app dialog instead of launching the browser.

### Changed

- **`enginePower` type corrected to `Int`** — the BMS reports motor power as a whole-number kW value; the model field was previously widened to `Double` unnecessarily. Decimal precision is only meaningful for `chargingPower`, which originates from a separate telemetry path and remains `Double`. All redundant `.toInt()` / `.toDouble()` conversions and the now-unreachable `.isFinite()` guard have been removed.
- **Off-state keepalive redesigned** — the redundant in-service 3-minute MCU wake loop has been removed; only the alarm-based `OffStateKeepaliveReceiver` remains. Its interval has been increased from 4 minutes to 90 minutes, allowing the MCU to genuinely sleep between pokes (~75 min idle per cycle) and significantly reducing overnight 12V / HV standby drain. `POWER_CONNECTED` remains the primary wake-up trigger for charging detection, so a charge session started by plugging in is detected immediately; the 90-minute alarm is a fallback backstop only. During active off-state charging sessions, WiFi is kept alive via a targeted keepalive every 10 minutes so that charging telemetry is not interrupted when the car is off.
- **Service self-stops when idle** — after the car has been off and not charging for 5 minutes, the telemetry service stops itself and releases the CPU wake lock, allowing the infotainment to enter deep sleep. This eliminates the overnight HV→12V DC-DC cycling that was observed as a steady SoC drain while parked. The 90-minute alarm restarts the service briefly for a periodic snapshot, then the self-stop fires again. Charging sessions are unaffected: the service stays alive for the full duration of any active charge.
- **Idle poll interval extended to 5 minutes** — when the car is off and not charging, the telemetry loop now ticks every 5 minutes instead of every 30 seconds, reducing unnecessary CPU wake-ups and repository calls. The AC/slow-charging case retains 30-second polling for SoC granularity. The MQTT settings hint has been updated to reflect the new publish cadence: driving at the configured interval, charging every 30 s, idle: service sleeps (no publish).
- **SCREEN_OFF keepalive removed** — the dynamically registered `SCREEN_OFF` receiver that called `McuWakeHelper.keepAlive()` on every screen-off event has been removed. It was firing at the exact moment the system tried to sleep, resetting the MCU WiFi countdown and preventing deep sleep. WiFi continuity during charging is now handled solely by the targeted 10-minute keepalive in the telemetry loop.
- **12V history chart Y-axis fixed** — the voltage axis is now always 12.0–14.0 V (previously 11.0–14.0 V). The SoC right-hand axis ticks were updated from five labels (0/25/50/75/100 %) to three (33/66/100 %) to match the three voltage gridlines.

### Fixed

- **BYD M6 cell count** — Now the number of cells is correct, which means SoH will be calculated correctly
- **12V chart crosshair showing `%.2f` literally** — the 12V voltage value in the crosshair tooltip was not being formatted; it now correctly displays the numeric value (e.g. `13.70 V`).

### Changed

- **Battery stat card** — title is now always "Battery"; value shows "SoH: X.X%" with one decimal place; subtitle shows the average battery temperature ("Temp: X.X °C") when available.
- **PHEV false-positive detection** — sentinel values (≥ 100,000) returned by BEV firmware for fuel-system fields are now excluded from the PHEV signal count; `getEnergyMode` is also excluded for confirmed BEV models, preventing BEV cars from being mis-classified as PHEVs during the compatibility probe sweep.
- **Motor RPM detailed chart** — the fullscreen/detailed view now uses peak-only bucketing at 480-point resolution (4× the condensed thumbnail) so the front motor shows a natural flat-zero baseline with real engagement spikes, rather than an artificially dense spike pattern. The condensed dashboard thumbnail is unchanged.
- **AWD motor stat card** — subtitle now shows total drive power only (e.g. "AWD · 187 kW") instead of a fabricated per-axle split that assumed a fixed front/rear ratio regardless of driving conditions.
- **Duration format** — minutes are now shown as "min" instead of "m" across trip history, trip detail, charging history, charging detail, and trip compare to avoid ambiguity with meters. Hours format updated accordingly (e.g. "1h 19min").

Previously, if one mode had data but the other did not, the mode-less lane rendered as a solid grey bar. Now each lane is only shown when it has at least one recorded data point with a known mode value.
- **Background poll rate** — when the car is off, the telemetry poll interval is reduced from 1 s to 30 s, significantly reducing CPU active time and 12V battery drain during overnight parking and long AC charging sessions. An intermediate 5 s tier applies when the SDK has sent events within the last 60 s (e.g. a remotely-awoken car sitting in Park), keeping mode changes visible promptly without continuous fast polling. DC fast charging (above 23 kW) retains 1 s polling for accurate power curve resolution. The service remains always-on as a foreground service; no data or functionality is lost. The delay now precedes the poll work and is based on the previous tick's state, eliminating any unnecessary fast poll on a car-on to car-off transition and maximising idle sleep time.
- **Regen / Driving Dynamics stat card** — renamed from "Regen / Driving Mode"; road grade (slope in degrees) is now shown in the subtitle when available, relocated from the Environment card where it did not fit contextually.
- **Drive/regen mode prompt** — the "Regen / Driving Dynamics" stat card on the dashboard now shows a hint when the car is on but either mode has not yet been detected, prompting the user to tap a mode on the car display. Trip Detail also shows a persistent info chip above the mode timeline when a saved trip is missing drive or regen mode data, with a message explaining how to enable recording on future trips.

### Fixed

- **Drivetrain mode label (AWD / FWD / RWD) always blank** — on some firmware builds the drivetrain state getter returns 0 when polled outside an active event callback. The app now falls back to the car catalog drivetrain type when the getter returns 0, so the motor stat card shows the correct AWD / FWD / RWD label throughout the drive.
- **Road slope reading 10× too steep** — the raw SDK value is in tenths of a degree but was being treated as whole degrees, so a barely noticeable descent would display as e.g. −6° instead of −0.6°. The value is now correctly divided by 10 at the read site and displayed with one decimal place.
- **Range projection chart changes after navigating away and back** — when returning to the dashboard from another screen (or after the app process was recreated), the range projection chart could gain an orange "Projected (actual)" line that was not visible during the live drive session before navigation. This happened because the trip restore path always assigned a projected value to every data point, including those below the calibration threshold. The restore path now leaves unstabilised points without a projected value, matching live session behaviour.

## [2.0.0] - 2026-Apr-28

### Added

- **Standalone telemetry runtime** — the app now reads vehicle data directly from the BYD DiLink SDK without requiring Electro or an MQTT broker. All dashboard, trip recording, and charging session features run from the built-in runtime path.
- **Physics-based energy breakdown** — Trip Detail Analysis tab shows how trip energy splits across rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag, elevation gradient (climb / descent), and auxiliary losses (12 V system, HVAC, residual). The model uses Crr = 0.0074 (calibrated against real trips, consistent with ISO 28580 for LFP EV tyres), CdA and kerb mass from the car catalog, and drivetrain efficiency η = 0.88. Components are scaled proportionally so they always sum to the measured consumed energy.
- **ABRP integration** — live telemetry can be forwarded to A Better Route Planner via the Link Generic API using a user-provided token. Includes a test-upload action and token visibility toggle. Opt-in only — disabled by default.
- **Outbound MQTT integration** — live telemetry can be published to any external MQTT broker (e.g. HiveMQ or HomeAssistant) using an Electro-compatible JSON schema, a configurable interval, and a test-publish action. Opt-in only — disabled by default.
- **Connections tab** — dedicated Settings screen for managing ABRP and MQTT integrations, each with a summary card and last-sync timestamp.
- **Drive and regen mode analytics** — drive mode (Normal / Eco / Sport) and regen mode are recorded with every trip data point, shown as a colour-coded timeline chart in Trip Detail, and summarised in the Analysis tab.
- **Combined Power + SoC charging chart** — Charging Detail now includes a dual-axis view with an X-axis toggle between elapsed time and SoC percentage, making DC taper shape easy to read.
- **Per-trip custom DC charging cost** — Trip Detail can override the home-tariff cost estimate with the actual amount paid at a public charger.
- **Environmentals card** — the dashboard temperature card now shows ambient temperature alongside PM2.5 indoor/outdoor readings where the car exposes them.
- **App Diagnostics monitor** — a toggleable section in Settings → Data showing live CPU, RAM, thread count, and uptime with 60-second history charts and an integrated ADB shell runner for diagnostics.
- **Pre-update database backup** — a local database snapshot is created automatically before any APK update is installed.

## [1.4.2] - 2026-Mar-29

### Added

- **Seal U** — support is added for Seal U Design and Seal U Comfort.

### Fixed

- **Live Charging Session Visibility** — resolved an issue in the Charging History screen where active/live charging sessions were filtered out and hidden; active sessions are now correctly displayed and pinned to the top of the list.
- **Range projection spikes** — during sustained regen or heavy braking the projected range would spike to 500+ km then crash back down. Root cause: negative energy values (`enginePower < 0`) were being subtracted from the rolling consumption window, making the denominator collapse toward zero.
